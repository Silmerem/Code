Story of the day

The market has read “Operation Economic Outcast” as good news. Oil fell for a second day, dropping more than 3% to a one-week low — Brent about $90, WTI about $83 — on the reasoning that a president reaching for sanctions is a president who has stopped reaching for bombs. Treasury yields fell with it, the 10-year down seven basis points to 4.629%. Washington intended a squeeze and got a de-escalation trade. Whether that reading survives contact with the named-institution measures still due this week is the question the week turns on.

Economy 2

Yields fell for the reason the Treasury wanted, but not by the mechanism it built — and the doubters now have a famous name at the front.

  • Treasury yields fall as oil prices continue their slide this week
    CNBC25 Aug

    The 10-year dropped more than seven basis points to 4.629%. Worth being precise about cause: this is cheaper crude lowering inflation expectations, not the buyback programme, which does not begin until 9 September. Bessent has been trying to buy this move for a week and the Iran trade delivered it for free.

  • Stanley Druckenmiller leads the doubters who think Bessent's bond ploys will fail
    CNBC25 Aug

    The objection is arithmetic rather than ideological: buybacks change who holds the debt, not how much of it there is, and the deficit is heading for $2tn this year against a $40tn stock. Warsh’s Jackson Hole keynote comes on Friday, and it is now the more important of the two events.

AI 3

The drought ends, and on the day before Nvidia reports, its largest customer says it has built a chip that beats Nvidia's.

  • OpenAI says its Jalapeño chip bests Nvidia's and others'
    Axios25 Aug

    A 128-chip system, 1.7 exaFLOPS, 27TB of high-bandwidth memory, benchmarked on open-source models against Blackwell. OpenAI says it will not sell it to anyone else — which is the point. The company Nvidia just extended $105bn of credit to is demonstrating it can build the thing it is borrowing to buy.

  • OpenAI's upcoming Jalapeño chip looks like it'll be an inference beast
    The Register25 Aug

    The sceptical read of the same numbers, and the useful one: these are vendor benchmarks on inference workloads, not training, and inference is where custom silicon has always been easiest to win. Built with Broadcom, on the design first reported in June.

  • Has corporate AI spending reached an inflection point?
    Fortune25 AugNewsletter

    The question tonight’s results are really about. Wall Street expects roughly $92.3bn of quarterly revenue and $2.09 a share from Nvidia — about double a year ago — from a company worth more than $5tn and carrying the index. Set that against Saturday’s Atlanta Fed finding that nine executives in ten cannot yet see a productivity gain.

Iran War 3

A campaign announced with a Normandy metaphor, priced by the market as the end of the shooting.

  • Oil drops more than 3% as the US shifts to economic pressure, easing fears of renewed war
    CNBC25 Aug

    Brent around $90, WTI around $83, a one-week low and a second consecutive fall. Traders are treating sanctions as the alternative to strikes rather than the prelude to them — consistent with Bessent’s own line last week that maximum economic pressure means “likely there will not be a large-scale kinetic restart”.

  • Oil prices fall for a second day as the US shifts from Iran strikes to sanctions
    France 2425 Aug

    The detail that explains the shrug: for all the language, no sanctions have actually been imposed yet, no countries have been named and no timeline given. Markets are discounting an announcement, not a measure. That is a low bar for the rest of the week to clear or fall under.

  • Iran war latest: Tehran vows to 'neutralise the economic war'
    CNN25 AugLive blog

    Iran’s security chief threatened to halt the flow of oil through Hormuz altogether, and Tehran named 45 tankers it says broke its transit rules, threatening to confiscate their cargoes. Meanwhile the Iranian and Omani foreign ministers met to discuss a temporary joint shipping corridor — the threat and the negotiation running in parallel, as they have all month.
