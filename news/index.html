Story of the day

A third American aircraft carrier is on its way to Iran. The USS Theodore Roosevelt strike group left San Diego on Sunday to join the George H.W. Bush and the George Washington, followed a day later by the Makin Island force carrying more than two thousand Marines; a US official says the build-up gives Trump a wider menu of options. Set that against the diplomacy of eight days ago — a seven-day plan, mediators in Doha, an argument narrowed to sequencing — and the military timetable now runs past the American midterms, which is where officials have already told the Wall Street Journal the bombing resumes.

AI 2

The accord has a name and a text now, and the companies in it are planning their own standards body.

  • AI’s biggest players promise to police themselves at the White House
    Fortune1 Oct

    The Joint Commitment on Frontier Responsibilities, in detail: internal controls covering cybersecurity, biosecurity and chemical threats, an undertaking that systems “do not hack or access technical systems in unintended ways”, outside auditors, board committees and regular meetings on shared standards. Google, OpenAI and Anthropic are separately working towards an industry-run standards body by early 2027. The clause about not hacking things is in there because models have been doing exactly that.

  • The next OpenAIs and Anthropics may come straight to the retail market
    CNBC1 Oct

    Rule changes that would let ordinary investors into assets previously reserved for institutions, arriving as the two largest private AI companies approach flotation at valuations near a trillion dollars each. Retail access to the upside is also retail exposure to the downside, and the downside here is a technology under federal investigation.

UK Politics 1

Around seven hundred prisoners walked out on Thursday under the early release scheme.

  • ‘Thank you Andy Burnham’: prisoners celebrate the first wave of the early release scheme
    ITV News1 Oct

    Some had served a third of their sentence, including for domestic abuse and some sexual offences; rape, serious child sexual offences, grooming and any unlawful killing are excluded. The Ministry of Justice promises the “toughest supervision in British history” — restriction zones, a large expansion of electronic monitoring, bans from pubs and public events. Nick Timothy for the Conservatives says the public will “pay the price”. The policy exists because the prisons are full, which is the part no one disputes.
