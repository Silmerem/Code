Story of the day

After six months of war, Tehran said out loud what its own numbers show. President Pezeshkian told state media that foreign trade has shrunk by more than a third under American sanctions and the naval blockade of Iranian ports, and that curbing inflation, creating jobs and weaning the country off the dollar are now the government’s central task. The figure underneath is starker: about 260,000 barrels a day loaded for export this month, against 1.7 million a year ago. The admission came with no concession attached — Iran will keep the strait, and keep talking.

One line, and it is the clearest measurement yet of what an economic war actually does.

  • Iran's leaders acknowledge the economic toll of war, pledge diplomacy and defence

Worth reading against Washington’s framing all week, which has been that sanctions will “collapse” the regime. Six months in, they have shrunk an economy without changing a position.

UK Politics 1

An ultimatum that ties the Falklands to a defence budget, and finally gives this brief a datable way into the Budget story.

  • US considers a new ultimatum to the UK over the Falklands
    Yahoo News UKest.28 Aug

    Washington is reported to be weighing withdrawal of its support for British sovereignty over the islands unless defence spending rises, with the Pentagon reviewing NATO members against the 5% pledge and the UK marked for “special attention”. The trigger: the Treasury confirmed on Friday that John Healey’s first Budget will not say when Britain reaches 3% of GDP on defence. London’s reply was that its position is “long-standing and unwavering”.

Marked est. because Yahoo News UK does not date its URLs and the copy places itself only as after Friday’s Treasury confirmation — so it is dated conservatively to Friday rather than Saturday. ITV News, the one reachable UK newsroom that datestamps, filed nothing on Saturday. This is also the first datable purchase this brief has had on the 28 October Budget, which it has been unable to run as a headline all week.

Iran War 2

Tehran is in no hurry, and it has just explained why it can afford not to be.

  • Iran war live: Tehran in no rush to reopen Hormuz
    Al JazeeraToday30 AugLive blog

    This morning’s thread, and the position is the same one Tehran has held since the memorandum lapsed on 17 August: the strait stays shut until the blockade lifts, assets are unfrozen and oil sanctions go. The Omani corridor talks continue in parallel. Also carried: Israeli settlers besieging the West Bank village of Qusra.

  • Iran trade falls as sanctions pressure the economy
    CNBC29 Aug

    The market read of the same admission. Kpler puts loadings at about 260,000 barrels a day this month against 1.7 million in August 2025 — a fall of more than 80%, and the strongest evidence that the naval blockade, rather than the sanctions announced with a Normandy metaphor last Monday, is what is actually biting.
