Story of the day

The pipeline that exists to make a closed Hormuz survivable is itself shut. Saudi Arabia’s East-West line runs about 750 miles from Abqaiq on the Gulf to Yanbu on the Red Sea, with a design capacity near seven million barrels a day after its expansions, and satellite images now show the damage done by drones launched from Iraq. Roughly 4% of world supply is off the board while it stays down. Brent closed 4.7% higher at $109.56 and the ten-year Treasury yield reached its highest since October 2023, hours before a Federal Reserve meeting that futures markets now price at a 92% chance of raising rates.

Finance 4

Equities down, oil up, yields at three-year highs, and a rate decision tomorrow that the market has already made.

  • Stocks slip as rising oil prices and rate-hike expectations dent sentiment
    CNBC14 SepLive updates

    The S&P 500 closed 0.48% lower at 7,619.98, the Nasdaq 0.56% down at 26,186.41 and the Dow off 152.09 points at 52,421.20. Fed funds futures put a quarter-point rise at roughly 92% when the committee finishes tomorrow, which would lift the target range above 3.5–3.75%.   • Saudi Arabia has shut a critical oil pipeline. Here’s why it matters for the global oil market
    CNN14 Sep

    The East-West line was the answer to the question “what if Hormuz closes”. With it down, there is no large-scale route out of the eastern Saudi fields that does not pass through the strait, and the market is pricing the loss of the alternative rather than the loss of the barrels.

  • Oil surges past $107 as a Hormuz attack and the Saudi pipeline shutdown rattle markets
    Euronews14 Sep

    Two shocks in one session: a vessel struck in the strait and the bypass route out of service. Euronews caught Brent above $107 during European hours and CNBC records a $109.56 settlement — the same day at two different moments, which is a fair picture of how fast this is repricing.

  • Wall Street’s AI doomsday trade is here: chipmakers sink while hyperscalers gain
    Fortune14 Sep

    Nvidia fell more than 3% while Meta, Microsoft and Alphabet rose. The split is the interesting part: investors are starting to separate the companies that need AI capital spending to continue forever from the ones that would make more money if it stopped.

Economy 1

Washington says the waterway is open. The futures curve says otherwise, and the gap between those two statements is worth an explainer.

  • The US says it’s clearing Hormuz traffic. Why are oil futures beyond $100?
    Al Jazeera14 SepExplainer

    Because a convoy is not an insurance policy. Traffic that moves under naval escort still cannot be underwritten at normal rates, and a cargo owner facing a war-risk premium and a possible total loss will pay up or stay home regardless of what a navy says about access. The price is measuring risk, not passage.

One headline. No reachable outlet filed dated economic data on 14 September — a Monday with no major release — and the Federal Reserve decision, which is the week’s real economic event, lands tomorrow.

AI 3

The president has picked a side in the industry’s argument with itself, and it is not the side asking to be slowed down.

  • Trump goes scorched earth on AI warnings, raging about data-centre opposition and regulation
    CNBC14 Sep

    Trump attacked calls for AI regulation and named Dario Amodei directly, warning of a “SICK conspiracy” against AI and data centres. Three days after the frontier labs publicly asked to be constrained, the person who would have to sign any constraint has said no in the strongest terms available to him.

  • Trump tells Americans not to ‘kill the golden goose’, rejecting voters’ turn against AI and data centres
    Fortune14 Sep

    The electoral half of the same speech. Local opposition to data centres — over power prices, water and land — has been building into something a midterm campaign has to answer, and the answer offered is that the objectors are endangering national prosperity.

  • AI infrastructure company Cornelis raises $205m to chip away at Nvidia’s dominance
    TechCrunch14 Sep

    Networking rather than chips: the bet is that what constrains a large training cluster is increasingly how fast its parts talk to each other. Modest money by this industry’s standards, and a useful reminder that the Nvidia position is being attacked at the joints rather than head-on.

Iran War 1

The war has moved off the water and onto the infrastructure built to route around the water.
