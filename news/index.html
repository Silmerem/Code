Story of the day

Kevin Warsh used his first Jackson Hole keynote to hand the blame back to his own institution. “The responsibility for 65 months of sustained, elevated inflation sits squarely with the central bank” — and the summer’s better readings, he said, do not tell him the underlying trend has improved. The standard he set is a high one: be confident inflation is falling “clearly and at sufficient speed. Otherwise, we have work to do.” Traders heard the last four words. Odds on a September rate rise jumped to 55.7% from about 35% the day before, and the two-year note leapt eight basis points.

Finance 2

A losing Friday inside a winning week, and the first week in three that the Dow has finished up.

  • Stocks wrap up a winning week even as inflation becomes top of mind
    CNBC28 Aug

    The S&P slipped 0.25% on Friday to 7,711.76 and the Nasdaq 0.52%, with Nvidia and Intel giving back part of Thursday’s surge. Over the week: S&P up 0.5%, Nasdaq 0.9%, Dow 0.5%. A market that absorbed a hawkish Fed chair and a record earnings night and moved less than 1% either way.

  • What we learned from Warsh's Jackson Hole speech
    CNBC28 Aug

    Mainly what he refused to give. No forward guidance, no reaction function — “you can call it an outline, you can call it a trail map, just don’t call it forward guidance”, which he says “has overstayed its welcome”. Markets have spent fifteen years being told what comes next. This chair intends to stop telling them.

Economy 4

The week's one scheduled event delivered, and it moved rates rather than shares.

  • Fed chairman Warsh warns on inflation at Jackson Hole
    CNBC28 Aug

    Sixty-five months of elevated inflation, and the blame placed on the central bank itself — an unusual thing for its chairman to say in his first keynote. The two-year note rose almost eight basis points to 4.31%, its highest since late July, and equities managed to rise anyway.

  • Warsh: interest rate increases are in play if inflation doesn't fall
    Axios28 Aug

    The plainest statement of the consequence. September’s meeting is 19 days away and the market now puts a hike at better than even — the first time this year the base case has been tightening rather than holding. Wednesday’s core PCE at 3.3% is the number that will decide it.

  • Warsh warns inflation is too high, sparking bets that rate hikes are coming
    NPR28 Aug

    Worth reading for what the speech means beyond the trade. A rate rise now would land on households already holding back — real spending was flat in July — and on a Treasury that is trying to lower its own borrowing costs. Warsh has just told both that the Fed will not be helping.

  • Treasury yields tread water ahead of Warsh's Jackson Hole speech
    CNBC28 Aug

    Included for the contrast. Yields sat still all Friday morning, then moved sharply the moment he spoke. For a fortnight the Treasury secretary has been buying bonds to bring long rates down; the Fed chair shifted them further in twenty minutes, in the opposite direction, using only words.

AI 3

The open-source world is being bought, one company at a time, by the people it was meant to compete with.

  • Open-weight AI companies are the Valley's hottest acquisition targets
    TechCrunch28 Aug

    Three deals in a fortnight: Stripe buying OpenRouter for more than $7bn, Nvidia paying about $6bn for the open-weight builder Poolside, and Nvidia now reported to be closing on Hugging Face. The movement this brief reported yesterday — American firms shifting spend towards open and Chinese models — is being answered by acquisition rather than by product.

  • What Hugging Face means for Nvidia
    Fortune28 AugNewsletter

    A reported $12.9bn for the hub where most open-source models are published and downloaded. Two motives, both defensive: it protects the chip franchise by owning the distribution layer for models that might otherwise run anywhere, and it puts Nvidia back into the cloud business it left to its own customers.

  • Nicola Coughlan and Hugh Bonneville among stars backing a campaign against AI voice cloning
    ITV News28 Aug

    Save Our Voices Now, with Matt Lucas also signed up. The consumer end of a section that otherwise reads as chip deals and capital: performers arguing that a voice is property and that consent should be required before it is copied. British law currently offers them very little.

UK Politics 1

A small, concrete piece of government, of the kind this administration keeps saying it prefers.

  • Burnham is planning to stop 'cowboy builders' — but how will it work?
    ITV News28 Aug

    An escrow app, Trusted Payment, going live next week: customers link payments to a project and money is released only as work is completed. More than 100,000 traders are meant to have access by the end of September, with the Approved Codes Scheme fully in place by the end of the year. The catch is in the detail — the scheme was designed by, and the app is run by, the private company Trusted Payments.

The state of play is unchanged: no US–Iran talks, the naval blockade squeezing Iranian exports, and Tehran holding the strait shut until the blockade lifts, its assets are unfrozen and oil sanctions go.

A quiet day in a war is worth printing as one. Padding this section with Wednesday’s stories would suggest something happened on Friday that did not.
