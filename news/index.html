Story of the day

Three central banks, one oil price, three different answers. The Federal Reserve raised rates on Wednesday and the ECB did so a week earlier; yesterday the Bank of England held at 3.75% on a 6–3 vote, with three members wanting a quarter point. Andrew Bailey’s warning was explicit: “the longer this volatility persists, the bigger the impact it will have on inflation, and the more likely it is we will need to raise Bank Rate”. Britain is not disagreeing about the inflation. It is betting that an economy struggling for growth cannot take the cure before it has to. Markets, meanwhile, took the day back: the S&P rose 1.14%, the Nasdaq 1.69%, and the ten-year yield eased below 5% again.

Finance 2

A day of relief in every direction: yields down, oil down, technology up.

  • Stocks surge as oil and bond yields retreat, recovering from the Fed-induced sell-off
    CNBC17 SepLive updates

    The Dow added 316.14 points, or 0.61%, to 51,778.04, with the S&P 500 up 1.14% at 7,637.76 and the Nasdaq 1.69% at 26,418.30. The ten-year yield fell back below 5%, to around 4.93%, while the thirty-year held at 5.348%. Equities recovered a day’s losses; the long end of the curve has not recovered a month’s.

  • Oil prices fall as Saudi Arabia reportedly offers more crude via Hormuz after the pipeline attack
    CNBC17 Sep

    Brent slipped to $105.81 and WTI to $102.14. The mechanism is worth seeing: ship-to-ship transfers in the Gulf of Oman have risen to 2.7 million barrels a day from 1.5 million in August, which is what “routing around a broken pipeline” physically looks like — more cargo moving through the strait everyone spent the summer trying to avoid.

Economy 2

The Bank of England broke ranks, and told everyone it may not hold the line for long.

  • Bank of England holds rates at 3.75% in a 6–3 split vote as inflation hits a five-month high
    Euronews17 Sep

    Three of nine wanted 4% immediately. A 6–3 hold with a governor saying a rise is becoming more likely is not really a decision to wait; it is a decision to wait until November, with the Budget in between. UK inflation reached 3.1% in August, its highest in five months.

  • Bank of England defies the Fed’s rate-hike lead, leaving rates unchanged
    CNBC17 Sep

    The divergence read from the outside. Britain now has the loosest policy of the three major Western central banks and the most exposure to imported fuel costs, which is a combination that usually shows up in the currency before it shows up anywhere else.

AI 3

A two-trillion-dollar valuation, an argument about regulation that has turned tribal, and a quieter fight over who gets the electricity.

  • Anthropic priced above $2 trillion as markets await an IPO filing
    Euronews17 Sep

    Pre-IPO trading is valuing the company at more than double its last private round, with Goldman Sachs, JPMorgan and Morgan Stanley reported to be leading an offering expected to raise over $60bn. Read it against Saturday: the company arguing loudest that the industry should slow down is being priced by the market as though it will not.

  • As AI chief executives clash over regulation, a new culture war is brewing
    Fortune17 SepAnalysis

    The week’s argument, mapped: Dario Amodei and Sam Altman on one side wanting independent evaluators and common standards, Mark Zuckerberg and Jensen Huang on the other rejecting the premise, with Huang insisting safety and speed are not in conflict. What began as a technical dispute about evaluation is hardening into the kind of identity fight that no longer resolves on evidence.

  • Google, Nvidia and Anthropic want Emerald AI to find space on the grid for more data centres
    TechCrunch17 Sep

    The AI Energy Management Alliance argues that if data centres agree to cut consumption on demand, as much as 100 gigawatts of new capacity could connect without new generation being built. It is the same companies quarrelling about safety agreeing readily about power — and it lands in a month when electricity costs are already a live political grievance.

Iran War 3

The president says the end is near; the map of where Saudi oil can physically go says otherwise.

  • Trump says the US is ‘hopefully’ nearing the end of the Iran war as Saudi Arabia and the Houthis trade strikes
    CNBC17 Sep

    Seven months in, and the claim arrives on a day when a third party to the war — Saudi Arabia — is exchanging fire with a fourth. “Hopefully” is carrying a great deal of weight, and nothing in the week’s reporting identifies a negotiation that would produce the ending.

  • From Yanbu to Sohar: tracking Saudi Arabia’s alternative oil routes
    Al Jazeera17 SepExplainer

    The most useful piece of the day: a route-by-route account of what the kingdom can actually use with the East-West line down. The short answer is that the alternatives are smaller, slower and in several cases within reach of the same weapons, which is why the market did not simply shrug at the pipeline.

  • How a Saudi pipeline attack is putting Europe’s oil security to the test
    Euronews17 Sep

    Yanbu is the Red Sea end of that pipeline, and the Red Sea is the route to Europe. A strike in the Saudi desert therefore lands on European refineries rather than Asian ones. The ECB had already raised rates on 10 September, before this attack, so it is not the cause of that decision — but it is the kind of event that makes the next one harder to avoid.
