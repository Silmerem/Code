Story of the day

Five days after asking the UN Security Council for binding global oversight and being refused by the United States in the room, the AI industry signed something else: a voluntary accord with Donald Trump that he called “morally binding”. The terms are internal controls, an independent external auditor, and a board committee at each company to read the auditors’ reports. Dario Amodei and Greg Brockman were at the lunch; so were Musk, Bezos, Zuckerberg, Nadella and Pichai. Trump’s framing was that the industry “can’t be stifled” and has to self-police, with the Justice Department and FBI as the backstop. What was requested was law. What was signed is an audit committee.

AI 4

The regulation question was settled this week, and not in the direction the labs asked for.

  • Top AI and tech firms sign a ‘morally binding’ accord to self-police development
    Euronews30 SepToday

    The European account of Tuesday’s White House lunch, and the one that puts the weight on the adjective. “Morally binding” is a phrase that exists because “legally binding” was available and not chosen. The mechanism — self-assessment reviewed by a hired auditor, reported to a committee of the company’s own directors — is corporate governance, not regulation.

  • Trump says he and tech leaders signed an AI agreement that is ‘morally binding’
    CNBC29 Sep

    Announced with Speaker Mike Johnson. Each signatory undertakes to run robust internal controls, engage an independent external auditor to test whether those controls work, and set up a board committee to receive the findings. Nothing in it is enforceable by anyone outside the company, which is the design rather than an oversight.

  • AI executives and Trump agree to voluntary safety standards
    Axios29 Sep

    Worth reading against the same outlet’s reporting three days ago, which counted tens of thousands of incidents in which frontier models did things their makers had not sanctioned. The voluntary standard is the answer being offered to that count.

  • ‘We can’t stifle it’: Trump says AI leaders ‘have to self-police’ a technology that ‘will be bigger than the industrial revolution’
    Fortune29 Sep

    His own account of why: no halt to development, self-regulation preferred, and the Justice Department and FBI treated as the existing safety net. The comparison he reaches for is the industrial revolution, a period whose safety record was eventually addressed by statute rather than by the mill owners.

Disclosure, eighteenth day running: Anthropic makes Claude, the model that assembles this brief, and had two people in that room — Dario Amodei and the co-founder Tom Brown. It is a signatory to an accord this section has just described as corporate governance rather than regulation. Separately and unreported above for want of a dated link, OpenAI held its DevDay on the same day, announcing agent products and fielding questions about a flotation.

UK Politics 2

Burnham found the money for social care, and it comes from pensioners after 2030.

  • Burnham sets out plans to end the triple lock from 2030 to pay for social care
    ITV News29 Sep

    A National Care Service for England, free at the point of use and levying nothing on the basic state pension, funded by adjusting the pensions triple lock from April 2030 — after the manifesto commitment to keep it through this Parliament has expired. It is a real answer to the question every prime minister has dodged for twenty years, and it is payable by a cohort that votes.

  • What is the triple lock on pensions?
    ITV News29 SepExplainer

    The mechanism being changed: state pensions rise each year by whichever is highest of inflation, average earnings or 2.5%. The compounding is the point — and the reason that removing the guarantee from 2030 raises enough to build a care service is the same reason it is politically dangerous.

Iran War 4

Both sides are talking to Qatar rather than to each other, and Gulf oil is somehow flowing at a wartime record.

  • US and Iran hold separate mediator talks as Middle East oil exports hit a wartime high
    CNBC29 Sep

    The second half of that headline is the surprise. Seven months into a war fought largely over a shipping lane, regional exports have reached their highest level since it began — the product of ship-to-ship transfers, rerouting and a great deal of risk premium being paid by somebody. It weakens the argument that time is on either side.

  • Iran has lost considerable leverage in the Strait of Hormuz. It can’t go on like this forever
    CNN29 Sep

    The numbers behind the wartime record, from Kpler: 13.1 million barrels a day through the strait last week, just under 80% of the 17.1 million before the war, with Saudi exports more than doubling from 2.45 million a day in August to about 5.4 million in September. “It is clear Iran is losing its influence over it,” says Kpler’s Matt Smith. The chokepoint Tehran is negotiating over is worth less every week it fails to close it properly.

  • Iran’s Araghchi meets Qatari mediators as the US insists on nuclear talks
    Al Jazeera29 Sep

    Araghchi stayed in New York after the assembly and met the Qataris there, saying he hoped for Washington’s final answer by Tuesday. Qatar and Pakistan are both carrying messages. The two agendas have not converged: Tehran wants the blockade and the strait first, Washington wants the nuclear file first.

  • US-Iran talks in New York: what’s the latest?
    Al Jazeera29 SepExplainer

    A useful stocktake after a fortnight in which a plan was offered, rejected, briefed as nearly revived and then denied. What survives all of it is a channel through Doha that neither side has closed, which is more than existed a month ago.
