Finance 4

Crude did in one session what a fortnight of diplomacy could not: it priced in the deal not happening.

  • CNBC Daily Open: Markets caught between oil jitters and AI optimism
    CNBCToday11 Aug

    The two forces pulling equities in opposite directions this morning, named plainly: a 5% jump in crude on the fading Hormuz deal against a fresh wave of enthusiasm for AI after Meta’s model release. One is a tax on every economy, the other a bet on a handful of companies.

  • Stock market today: Live updates
    CNBC10 AugLive blog

    Monday ended almost exactly flat — S&P 500 down 0.06% at 7,753.11, Dow off 60.95 to 53,975.98, Nasdaq down 0.32% — which understates the day. Underneath, WTI closed about 5% higher at $82.13 and Brent at $87.72 as confidence in a near-term Hormuz settlement drained away.

  • Oil prices climb as Iranian demands cloud outlook for Strait of Hormuz
    Al Jazeera10 Aug

    The mechanism behind the move: Tehran’s list of preconditions turned a deal that traders had treated as days away into one with no timetable at all. Nearly a fifth of the world’s seaborne oil used to pass through the strait.

  • Oil prices rise as traders assess US-Iran talks on Strait of Hormuz deal
    Euronews10 Aug

    The European read on the same session, and the one that matters most for the eurozone: energy import costs feed straight into an inflation rate the ECB is already fighting, with no domestic production to cushion it.

AI 3

Meta published a 6,500-word argument for giving superintelligence away, and a model to go with it.

  • Zuckerberg envisions 'superintelligence' for everyone in AI manifesto
    France 24Today11 Aug

    “Rather than centralizing superintelligence, we should distribute it widely and give every person the ability to direct it.” The essay frames open weights as the American answer to Chinese open-source models. Future of Life’s Anthony Aguirre asks the obvious question back: what makes Meta think anyone could control something exponentially more capable than themselves?

  • Meta launches Muse Glimmer open-weight AI model
    CNBC10 Aug

    Glimmer is built to run on a personal computer rather than in a data centre, and Meta is opening the weights for Muse Spark 1.2 alongside it. On-device inference is the part with teeth: a model nobody has to rent is a model nobody can switch off.

  • Meta just picked a side in a big debate over the future of AI
    CNN Business10 Aug

    The context that makes this a policy story rather than a product launch: Meta is the one major Western lab that did not sign the EU’s code of practice for the AI Act, and Washington’s new framework covers only closed models. An open-weight release sits outside both regimes by design.

UK Politics 3

Clacton votes the day after tomorrow, and the national polling going into it cannot agree on whether Reform is first or third. All three dates are inferred, not published — see the note below.

  • Andy Burnham set for crunch cross-party talks to end Britain's care crisis – but snubs Nigel Farage
    GB News6 Augest.

    Badenoch and Davey invited, Farage not, after he attacked the plans as a revived “death tax”. Reform is polling level with Labour on most measures, which makes the exclusion a statement about who counts as a legitimate interlocutor.

  • Nigel Farage's Reform drops to third in bombshell poll as Labour and Tories storm ahead
    GB News3 Augest.

    Lord Ashcroft put Reform on 18%, their worst since the 2024 election, behind Labour on 27% and the Conservatives on 23% — sixteen seats on his projection. Reform called it an outlier; a YouGov poll days later had them back in front. Their average since Burnham took office is 24%.

  • Labour wipes out Reform UK poll lead as party enjoys 'Burnham bounce'
    Yahoo News UK3 Augest.

    More in Common, separately, had Labour on 28% and four clear. A spread on Reform running from 18% to 24% inside a fortnight is not a trend, it is a measurement problem — and Thursday will produce one real number against which all of it can be checked.

On Clacton itself, which no reachable outlet has covered inside the window in a way that can be dated: polls open on Thursday, Farage faces a record 33 other candidates, and Labour, the Conservatives, the Liberal Democrats, the Greens and Restore Britain are all declining to stand. Survation gives him 73% locally; a national Ipsos poll found more Britons would prefer Count Binface to win.

These three lines have now run for a third morning. Iran War 4

Two compensation claims pointing at each other, and a president saying the strait is already open while Iran says it will stay shut.

  • Iran war live: Trump claims Hormuz Strait open, seeks Iranian compensation
    Al JazeeraToday11 AugLive blog

    This morning’s thread, and the claim to watch: the president says the strait is open. Iran’s position, unchanged, is that it does not reopen until the blockade ends. Traffic figures rather than statements will settle which is true.

  • Trump demands compensation from Iran as talks on Strait of Hormuz continue
    Al JazeeraToday11 Aug

    The scope of the demand is the story: fifty years of damages, covering this war, attacks on US targets going back two decades, the families of protesters killed inside Iran, and harm done in Lebanon, Syria, Yemen and Gaza. It is not a number anyone expects to be paid.

  • Iran aims to put pressure on Trump amid negotiations with Oman over Strait of Hormuz
    NPR10 Aug

    Read as leverage rather than as terms, Tehran’s list makes more sense: it is calibrated to a president facing petrol prices and midterms. Foreign minister Araghchi is blunt that there are no negotiations — “an exchange of messages through intermediaries, but this is not called negotiations”.

  • Trump says he would require compensation from Iran as condition for talks
    CNN10 AugLive blog

    Monday’s thread, where the demand was first made at the White House. Setting compensation as a precondition for talks, against Iran setting it as a precondition for reopening, leaves the two positions structurally identical and mutually blocking.
