Story of the day

Iran has put a timetable on the table. Abbas Araghchi told reporters on the General Assembly sidelines that the Strait of Hormuz reopens on day six of a seven-day plan, with talks on a final nuclear deal starting on day seven. The price is the June memorandum Washington abandoned: about $12bn in frozen Iranian assets released, sanctions on Iranian oil lifted, the naval blockade ended. The Trump administration has already said it will not go back to those terms. Oil fell anyway — American crude down 2.33% to $92.41 — which is a market betting on a deal whose central condition one side has publicly ruled out.

Economy 1

A country asking who pays for the damage, at the assembly where that question is usually deferred.

  • Nepal’s leader labels a devastating flood a ‘warning to the world’
    Al Jazeera25 Sep

    At least 1,453 people died on 26 August when a glacial collapse sent mud, rock and water through northern Nepal and across the China-Tibet border. Balendra Shah has asked the international community to share the cost of adaptation and proposed a “Himalaya Climate Resilience Mechanism”. It is an economic demand dressed as a climate one: who funds the infrastructure of a country that did not cause the warming.

One headline. No statistical agency released anything dated 25 September, and the week’s trade news — the two-month truce extension — ran in yesterday’s edition.

Iran War 4

Seven days, four conditions, and an American refusal already on the record.

  • Iran pitches a seven-day roadmap to end the conflict with the US
    Al Jazeera25 Sep

    Araghchi’s sequence: four or five days of initial steps, the strait reopening on the sixth, negotiations on a final nuclear deal beginning on the seventh. It is the first Iranian offer of this war with dates attached rather than conditions alone.

  • What’s in Iran’s seven-day plan to reopen the Strait of Hormuz?
    Al Jazeera25 SepExplainer

    The asks in detail: at least $12bn of frozen assets released, sanctions on Iranian oil lifted, the naval blockade ended, and a return to the June memorandum that collapsed in early July. Every one of them is something Washington must do first, which is the plan’s structural weakness as a negotiating document.

  • Iran offers to reopen the Strait of Hormuz within seven days and restart nuclear talks
    CNBC25 Sep

    The market read. Oil fell on the offer despite the administration having said it will not return to the June terms, which suggests traders are pricing the existence of a process rather than the content of one. That is a reasonable bet after a fortnight in which the only alternative was escalation.

  • Iran proposes a plan to the US to reopen Hormuz in seven days and end the war
    Euronews25 Sep

    The European account, and the one that matters most for gas and shipping costs on this side of the Atlantic. Note what the plan does not contain: any Iranian concession that precedes an American one. Tehran is offering a sequence, not a trade.
