A week that opened in relief and closed in a correction.

  • AI boom hits a wall on Wall Street as OpenAI cuts its fundraising target and Meta gets a credit warning
    Fortune31 Oct

    Two separate signals pointing the same way. OpenAI reduced its round to $20 billion from $30 billion, per the Financial Times, and S&P Global moved Meta’s outlook to negative from stable over cash flow and spending. Equity investors can decline to buy. Lenders and rating agencies are the ones who decide what the building programme costs, and this week both started saying so.

UK Politics 2

The prime minister concedes his first Budget was not the one he wanted.

  • Andy Burnham admits his first Budget as prime minister was ‘not ideal’
    ITV News30 Oct

    “The room for manoeuvre wasn’t what I would have hoped it to be,” two days after a Budget that raised the bank surcharge to 6% for fifteen months, put £100 towards energy bills, extended the fuel duty cut to July 2027 and lifted the minimum wage 3.9%. He also ruled out an election in 2027. A prime minister who says the constraint beat him is making an argument about the next Budget, not this one.

  • Government working ‘flat out’ to reduce the welfare bill, PM says after cost warnings
    ITV News29 Oct

    The Office for Budget Responsibility expects sickness and disability benefits to approach £100 billion a year before the end of the decade. That number is the reason the previous headline exists: it is the single largest claim on the room for manoeuvre the prime minister says he did not have.

Both from ITV News, and both older than the rest of this edition, under this section’s wider window. It is the only datestamped British newsroom this brief can reach, and it published nothing political on Saturday.
