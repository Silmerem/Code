Story of the day

The August jobs report undid a week of argument in one line. Payrolls rose 162,000 against a consensus of 53,000, unemployment held at 4.1%, and June and July were revised up by 55,000 between them — the July decline this brief reported as a shock has been revised into a gain. A day after a Fed governor said he was inclined to hold, futures moved to a 58% chance of a rise from 49.4%. The Dow fell 272 points on good news, which is what happens when the economy’s strength is the thing the market is afraid of.

Economy 4

One number, four readings — and a demographic detail nobody expected.

  • US payrolls rose 162,000 in August, much more than expected; unemployment at 4.1%
    CNBC4 Sep

    Triple the forecast. Bars and restaurants led the gains; information-sector employment fell, which the report’s readers attribute to AI investment displacing jobs in exactly the industry that builds it. Women accounted for about 98% of the month’s total.

  • The US economy added 162,000 jobs in August, more than double expectations
    CNN4 Sep

    Worth reading beside its own preview from the day before, which set up a labour market “in the throes of a major transformation” and warned that 94% of three years’ job growth had come from three sectors. August did not break that pattern; it just delivered more of it than anyone forecast.

  • August hiring gives the Fed less reason to hesitate on a rate hike
    Axios4 Sep

    The blunt version. Twenty-four hours earlier the story here was a governor breaking ranks towards holding; a strong labour market removes the argument he was making. What has not changed is the oil price, which is doing the inflating and is set in the Strait of Hormuz.

  • US added 162,000 jobs in August, blowing past expectations
    The Hill4 Sep

    The Washington reading, and the political one: a strong labour market is what an administration wants in the middle of a war that has pushed petrol above $4 a gallon. The Hill’s URLs carry no date, but the story is Friday’s and is placed there by its own contents.
