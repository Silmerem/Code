Story of the day

Nvidia answered the bubble question with a number nobody expected. Data centre revenue of $89bn, up 117% on the year, guidance of $108bn against the $104bn the street wanted, and Jensen Huang forecasting 70% revenue growth for fiscal 2028 — roughly $100bn more than analysts had modelled. The stock rose more than 4% after hours. The finance chief met the circular-financing charge head on: “We know some will call this circular financing. We see it differently.” Saying it out loud is not the same as answering it.

Finance 2

An inflation print that changed nothing, and a results night that changed the shape of the week.

  • Core PCE in line, but not enough to lower Fed rate-hike expectations
    CNBC26 Aug

    Stocks flat to lower, Treasury yields little changed, September’s meeting no clearer. Morgan Stanley’s Ellen Zentner put it plainly: a “mild upside inflation surprise and relative economic strength weren’t necessarily what investors — or the Fed — wanted to see”. In a week built around three events, the first one landed and moved nothing.

  • Nvidia unleashes a 70% growth bombshell and defends against 'circular financing' doomsayers
    Fortune26 Aug

    The numbers under the headline: hyperscaler revenue more than doubled to $48.7bn, and AI cloud, industrial and enterprise customers — the part of the business that is not the big five — grew 138% to $40.3bn. That second figure is the real defence against the bubble charge, because it is demand from outside the circle.

Economy 2

Prices in line, spending stalled. The Fed's problem did not get easier on Wednesday.

  • The Fed's preferred inflation gauge shows core prices rose 3.3% annually in July
    CNBC26 Aug

    Core PCE up 0.2% on the month and 3.3% on the year, both matching estimates exactly. In-line is not the same as good: 3.3% is well above the 2% target, and it is the fourth month running that the gauge has refused to fall further. Rate-hike odds for September were untouched.

  • Consumers pulled back on spending in July in the face of continuing price pressures
    CNN26 Aug

    The overlooked half of the same release. Adjusted for inflation, spending was flat in July after a 0.4% gain in June — income rose 0.4% and nominal spending only 0.2%, so households took the money and held it. A consumer that stops spending while prices keep rising is the combination neither the Fed nor the Treasury has an instrument for.

On investing in the labs he sells to: a “once-in-a-generation opportunity”, and “the only regret I have is that I didn’t invest more and sooner”. The risk critics name — that trouble at one customer ripples through the whole structure — is not addressed by growing faster.

Iran War 3

A third straight fall in oil, and the reason is not the sanctions — it is Oman.

  • Iran and Oman agree on a temporary Hormuz route: what we know
    Al Jazeera26 Aug

    A joint statement from the two foreign ministers: a framework for a temporary navigational corridor through the strait, and a joint project to clear it of mines. First concrete movement on the waterway since the memorandum lapsed on 17 August — and it came from the country Trump threatened to bomb a week ago.

  • US crude hovers around $80 as oil sinks on Hormuz deal hopes
    CNBC26 Aug

    Brent down 2.5% to $86.38, WTI down 2.2% to $80.52, a third consecutive session lower. Crude has now given back everything the expired deadline added. The market is pricing the Omani corridor, not the American campaign — which is an unflattering verdict on which of the two it thinks will move ships.

  • Iran war latest: Tehran says Washington is the obstacle to a Hormuz deal
    CNN26 AugLive blog

    The gap between the announcement and the action, now visible: Monday’s package named no countries, set no timeline, and pointedly did not include secondary sanctions on the large Chinese banks that handle Beijing’s Iranian oil purchases. “The greatest financial offensive ever” has so far left the single largest lifeline untouched.
