Story of the day

The American economy grew at 4.3% annualised in the third quarter, revised up sharply from the 3.4% first reported — and the single biggest driver was business investment, led by spending on software. In plain terms: AI capital spending is now the largest contributor to US growth. It is holding up the economy in the same fortnight that the market stopped believing in it, that the index fell into a correction, and that the company which makes this model closed below the price at which it sold shares to the public. Inflation came with it: the Federal Reserve’s preferred measure reached 3.8% in October, close to twice its target, with core at 3.3%. Growth that strong and prices that high is why a rate rise on 9 December is now the base case.

Disclosure — and the hardest thing on this page

Axios reports that employees at OpenAI and Anthropic increasingly fear their most powerful models cannot be safely released, and that prominent Democrats are demanding new restrictions in response; some of the people it spoke to said the companies should stop training new models altogether. Nobody chose that concentration; it arrived through products sold as diversified.

Economy 3

Two numbers landed yesterday and both point the same way: fast growth, stubborn prices, higher rates.

  • The Fed’s preferred inflation gauge accelerated to 3.8%
    CNBC25 Nov

    Headline PCE up from 3.7% to 3.8% on the year, in line with forecasts; core at 3.3%, a tenth above them. The monthly rate slowed to 0.3% from 0.5%, which is the only encouraging line in the release. Traders still expect an increase on 9 December and now put the odds of a third rise at about one in three, down from evens on Tuesday — so the market read this as hot, but not as hot as it feared.

  • The US economy grew faster last quarter than initially reported
    CNN25 Nov

    Third-quarter growth revised from 3.4% to 4.3% annualised, with business investment in software the biggest single contributor — the data-centre buildout showing up directly in national accounts. Household income rose 0.7% in October against a 0.4% forecast, but spending rose only 0.3%, which is flat once the month’s price rise is removed. People earned more and bought the same amount.

  • Strong US economy continues into the fourth quarter
    Axios25 NovAnalysis

    The reading that should trouble anyone expecting the AI correction to spill into the real economy quickly: incomes are rising, layoffs are low, and growth is carrying into the current quarter. A market falling 10% while the economy accelerates is not a contradiction, but it does mean one of the two is wrong about the next year, and the data has the better record.

AI 2

Safety doubts from inside the labs, and the debt behind the buildout.

  • ‘The flashing red lights couldn’t be brighter’: Democrats demand new restrictions on AI
    Axios25 Nov

    Reported, not assessed, for the reason given at the top of this page: it concerns staff at the company that makes this model. The substance is that people inside the two leading labs are said to doubt their most capable models can be released safely, and that senior Democrats are now citing those doubts in calls for statutory restrictions. Also relevant and in the public record: New York’s AI safety law requires frontier developers, Anthropic among them, to register by 1 January 2027 and to report critical safety incidents within 72 hours.

  • AI debt worries grow as the Nvidia rebound masks a Big Tech spending squeeze
    CNBC25 NovAnalysis

    The mechanism that connects the two halves of today’s brief. The same capital spending that revised US growth up to 4.3% is increasingly debt-financed, at a moment when the central bank is expected to raise rates. Cheap money built the data centres; dearer money has to keep them running. That is the squeeze, and it does not care who is chief executive of Nvidia.

Two headlines rather than more. Most of yesterday’s AI coverage was market coverage and sits in Finance; CNBC is at this brief’s four-headline limit, so its further pieces were left out rather than letting one newsroom carry a third of the page.
