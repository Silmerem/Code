Story of the day

Brent crossed $100 for the first time since 23 July, closing at $102.05, up $2.20 on the day and roughly $35 on the year, after American forces sank five Iranian tankers and Iran fired eighteen ballistic missiles at a US air base in Jordan. What makes this different from the last spike is where it has landed: American diesel set an all-time record, the ten-year Treasury yield reached its highest since November 2023, and Wall Street fell for a third straight day. Yesterday the war was a price. Today it is an interest rate.

Finance 4

The US Treasury tried to hold the long end of the curve down and the bond market went straight through it anyway.

  • Treasury to buy back up to $6bn in longer-term debt, triple the normal level
    CNBC9 Sep

    Aimed at ten- and twenty-year notes, the thinnest part of the market and the part where yields have hit multi-year highs. It did not work: the ten-year rose to 4.857%, its highest since November 2023, because parts of Wall Street had been positioned for $7bn or $8bn and read the smaller number as a limit rather than a floor.

  • Stock futures inch higher as traders brace for the wholesale inflation report
    CNBC9 SepLive updates

    The Dow closed Wednesday down 405.41 points, or 0.77%, at 52,380.66; the S&P 500 fell 0.48% to 7,636.36 and the Nasdaq 0.64% to 26,253.34 — a third consecutive decline for all three. August producer prices land this morning, with a Dow Jones consensus of +0.3% on the month and +5.3% on the year, and consumer prices follow tomorrow.

  • Brent crude surpasses $100 a barrel as Iran and the US escalate attacks
    Al Jazeera9 Sep

    Up nearly 20% since the middle of last week, on a strait that has been largely shut since February. The number to hold on to is not $102 but the rate of change: this is a war premium being repriced upward in days, not a level the market has settled on.

  • ‘I am the house now’: Bessent dares currency markets to move against him
    Fortune9 Sep

    The Treasury Secretary’s framing of his own interventions, delivered on the same day the bond market ignored his buyback. Worth filing next to the yield move above: a finance minister publicly betting he can outlast traders is a claim that gets tested rather than accepted.

Economy 2

The barrel price stopped being an abstraction the moment it reached the pump and the freight bill.

  • Oil surges back above $100 as diesel climbs to a record $5.94 a gallon
    Fortune9 Sep

    Petrol rose seven cents overnight to $4.22 a gallon, more than a dollar above this time last year, and jet fuel has become expensive enough that American and international carriers are cutting flights while raising fares. Diesel is the one that matters for the inflation prints: it is the input cost under almost every delivered good in the country.

  • Oil surges past $100 a barrel again as US-Iran clashes intensify
    Euronews9 Sep

    The European read on the same move, and the more consequential one this week: the ECB decides on rates today into an inflation rate that is 3.3% almost entirely because energy inflation is 14.3%. A central bank cannot raise a strait open, which is the argument it has to answer this afternoon.

Two headlines. The August producer price index and the ECB decision both land today rather than yesterday, so they belong in tomorrow’s edition rather than being reported here before they exist; searches for other economic data filed on 9 September returned nothing inside the window.

AI 2

Two pieces asking the same awkward question from opposite ends: if the technology is arriving as promised, why is the money getting harder rather than easier?

  • The latest ‘crack in the thesis’ for the trillion-dollar AI boom: tokens are getting cheaper
    Fortune9 Sep

    The effective price American businesses pay per million tokens has fallen about 41% from its March peak, from $1.15 to 68 cents, after OpenAI cut GPT-5.6 Luna by 80% and Anthropic cut its own prices last month. Cheaper inference is unambiguously good for buyers; it is a problem for anyone who justified the capital spending by assuming the revenue per token would hold.

  • AGI is here, Nvidia’s Jensen Huang has declared. Here’s why the market doesn’t care
    Fortune9 SepAnalysis

    The strongest possible claim about the technology, made by the person with the most to gain from it being believed, and priced by investors at approximately nothing. Read alongside the piece above, the market’s indifference looks less like scepticism about capability and more like a judgement about who captures the value.

Both from one outlet, which is the section limit and not a preference. TechCrunch, The Register, Axios, CNBC and Euronews were all searched; nothing they filed on 9 or 10 September qualified, and Axios’s story on Anthropic quitting a tech lobby group over chip-export bills is from 8 September, one day outside the window.

Iran War 4

Five tankers sunk, eighteen missiles intercepted over Jordan, and a president saying out loud that the war ends after the midterms.

  • US military says it destroyed five Iranian oil tankers after attacks on a Navy warship
    NPR9 Sep

    CENTCOM named the vessels — the Kivik, Charminar, Horizon 1, Riesco and Derya — four struck in the Gulf of Oman and one near Kharg Island, with all five crews warned to abandon ship first. The trigger was two Iranian attempts in two days on the same US warship, which CENTCOM says evaded both without casualties.

  • US destroys five Iranian tankers; Iran retaliates with attacks on a Jordan base
    Al Jazeera9 Sep

    Iran’s answer was Muwaffaq Salti air base, used by US forces. Jordan says its air defences intercepted eighteen ballistic missiles and two more fell in uninhabited ground, with no casualties. Jordan has been struck repeatedly since the war began on 28 February and is not a party to it.

  • Iran and US strike tankers in the biggest shipping attack since the war began
    France 249 Sep

    The measurement that makes yesterday distinct rather than merely another bad day: by tonnage attacked, this is the largest single exchange against commercial shipping in six months of fighting. The escalation is no longer about which navy controls the strait but about how much cargo each side is willing to destroy to prove it.

  • Trump says the Iran war ‘will end immediately’ after the US midterm elections
    Al Jazeera9 Sep

    Asked about restarting nuclear talks, he said: “We’re not looking for it, to be honest.” The remark concedes a timetable set by an election rather than by conditions on the water — and the politics behind it are visible in the polling, which has support for the war at 31%, down from 37% in March.
