Story of the day

Trump has ruled out attacking Iran before the midterms on 3 November. Read the sentence he did not say: nothing about afterwards. Oil had run to about $105 during Thursday on an Atlantic report that the White House had asked the Pentagon for strike options usable before the vote, and the statement took it off the top; the blockade of Iranian ports, he added, “will remain in full force and effect”. The Wall Street Journal has reported him telling aides he expects to resume bombing after the election, and he has threatened exactly that in public. So the war has not been given an end. It has been given a date, three and a half weeks out, and both sides now have a reason to run the clock down rather than settle anything.

Finance 2

A day when both of the market’s two drivers moved on a single president’s sentences.

  • US stocks slide as oil prices fluctuate over renewed Iran war fears
    Al Jazeera8 Oct

    Crude up about 4% to around $104 on the strike-options report, then back off the highs once Trump said there would be no attack before the election, with equities lower on both the war and the AI story below. A rare thing worth noting: this is a correctly datable account of a US session, which the market live blogs this brief normally relies on are not.

  • Treasury yields are lower after reaching multiyear highs as traders weigh the latest bond auction
    CNBC8 Oct

    The 10-year down five basis points to 5.227% and the 30-year down six to 5.601%, after a $22 billion sale of 30-year paper drew solid demand. Second auction this week to go well, and the second retreat from a 24-year high that followed one. The long end is currently being set by the auction calendar more than by the Fed.

Economy 2

Nobody is being laid off, and nobody is being hired either.

  • Labor Department notes historically low layoffs
    Axios8 Oct

    Weekly claims have eased to about 198,000, the low of the series, and announced job cuts in September were down a fifth on a year earlier according to Challenger. Hold that against last Friday’s 29,000 new jobs and you have the shape of this labour market: employers are not firing, they are declining to hire. That is a worse place for anyone looking for work than a market with churn in it.

  • Treasury yields are ‘really, really high’ but can come down soon, Bessent’s new adviser says
    CNBC8 Oct

    David Zervos, now counsellor at the Treasury, argues the long end falls once the energy shock from the Iran war resolves. The argument is coherent and the conflict is worth naming: the official who would most like yields lower is the one forecasting that they will be. His case rests entirely on a war ending, which the lede above has just had postponed by at least a month.

AI 4

OpenAI’s revenue turned out to be $20 billion smaller than the number everyone had been using, and the reason it was bigger involves this brief’s own maker.

  • OpenAI annualized revenue $20 billion less than previously reported
    Axios8 Oct

    About $50 billion annualised at the end of September, against the roughly $70 billion widely reported. The explanation is an accounting choice, not a collapse: the larger figure grossed up partner revenue so that it could be set beside Anthropic’s, because Anthropic counts sales made through its cloud partners and OpenAI does not. Axios’s closing point is the one that will matter at an IPO — public investors want revenue, not a construct built for comparing startups.

  • Nvidia, Oracle, CoreWeave and other AI stocks sink on the OpenAI revenue report
    CNBC8 Oct

    Nvidia down 3%, AMD and Broadcom 4%, Super Micro nearly 5%, Intel 5%, Oracle nearly 6%, CoreWeave nearly 8%. None of those companies published anything yesterday. They fell because a private customer’s revenue figure was restated, which is a precise measure of how much of the chip and cloud complex is now priced off one buyer’s spending plans.

  • Anthropic changes usage policy to ban model abuse and election interference
    TechCrunch8 Oct

    New prohibitions written into the usage policy covering election interference, weapons software and surveillance, four weeks before an American election in which the company’s products are in wide use. A usage policy is a promise about enforcement rather than a technical control, so the thing to watch is not the wording but whether anyone is found to have broken it and what happens next. See the disclosure below.

  • Fortune Tech: Microsoft revamps Windows for agentic AI
    Fortune8 Oct

    The operating system rebuilt around agents that act rather than answer, including a feature to make sandboxing an agent easier. That last part is the admission inside the announcement: you do not ship sandboxing tools for software you expect to stay inside its own boundaries.

UK Politics 3

Labour held Starmer’s old seat, and the Greens took thirteen points off them doing it.

  • Sagal Abdi-Wali says Labour by-election victory is a testament to diversity
    ITV News9 OctToday

    Camden’s council leader takes Holborn and St Pancras with 12,292 votes to Zack Polanski’s 8,928, a majority of 3,364 on a turnout of 38.76% against 54.46% at the general election. In her victory speech she called it a win for diversity and warned that cohesion should not be “casually undone”; the business secretary described remarks made about her during the campaign as “straightforward racism”.

  • Greens concede in Holborn by-election, saying Polanski came ‘close second’
    Yahoo News UK9 OctToday

    Second place on 33% against Labour’s 45%, a thirteen-point swing from Labour to the Greens in a seat Labour has held since 1983. “Close second” is doing work, but the swing is the real number, and George Galloway’s late entry split exactly the vote Polanski needed. A party leader who takes thirteen points off the governing party and still loses has both an argument and a problem.

  • The by-election that will determine Zack Polanski’s future
    ITV News8 Oct

    Written before the count, and worth reading after it for the stakes it sets out: a former Green leader had suggested Polanski’s own leadership would be in question if he lost. He fought this while his party’s Brighton conference was passing a motion that “Zionism is racism”, on which he abstained — a position that satisfied neither side of it.

Iran War 4

A month’s reprieve announced, with the blockade kept and nothing promised past 3 November.

  • Trump rules out a new Iran attack before the US midterm elections
    France 249 OctToday

    Announced on Truth Social, alongside a claim of “productive discussions” with Tehran and no detail about them, and an explicit undertaking that the blockade of Iranian ports continues. The commitment covers twenty-five days.

  • Analysis: why Trump and Iran may now be waiting out the midterms
    CNN9 OctTodayAnalysis

    The incentive structure both sides now share. Tehran gains nothing by conceding to an administration that may be weaker in a month; Washington gains nothing by starting something it would have to finish during a campaign fought on fuel prices. Mutual delay is not peace, and it leaves the blockade, the closed strait and the Yemen front all running.

  • Trump says he will not attack Iran before the midterm election
    CNN8 Oct

    The report as it broke, and the context that makes it news: the Atlantic had just reported the White House asking the Pentagon for options usable before the vote. The statement is therefore a denial of a specific plan rather than a change of policy, and the president has not ruled out anything for the day after.

  • Iran war updates: Trump promises not to resume strikes before the midterms
    CBS News8 OctLive blog

    Two further moves on the same page: Vance appearing to soften the American position on Iranian nuclear demands, and Rubio saying Tehran has “lost control” of the Strait of Hormuz. Iran says it has received the US response to its seven-day plan and will reply within days, with enrichment its stated red line. A softening and a triumphal claim on the same day is how an administration talks when it does not yet know which it will need.
