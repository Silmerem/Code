Story of the day

The chief executives of the two largest AI labs sat before the United Nations Security Council and asked to be regulated. Dario Amodei told the fifteen members that “if managed poorly, I even believe AI could be a risk to humanity as a whole”; Sam Altman said humanity could “lose control of the future of AI” and that “the most important decisions cannot be made by labs in San Francisco alone”. The American representative in the room, Michael Kratsios, answered: “We totally reject all efforts by international bodies to assert centralised control and global governance of AI.” The session was convened by France. Two industries’ worth of lobbying usually runs the other way; here the companies asked for rules and the government refused on their behalf.

Economy 4

The summit produced the small step everyone predicted: two more months.

  • Trump-Xi summit: four key takeaways from the Washington meeting
    Al Jazeera24 Sep

    Scott Bessent announced the trade truce — lower US tariffs in exchange for Chinese rare-earth and critical-mineral exports — is extended by two months, from mid-November to 10 January. The underlying rates are untouched: Chinese goods still face 36.5% entering the US and American goods 31% entering China.

  • Trump and Xi to talk Taiwan, AI, trade and Iran as the Washington summit kicks off
    CNBC24 Sep

    Day two of a three-day visit, with the agenda spanning the four things the two governments disagree about most. Trump called it a “great meeting”. Sector-specific tariff relief, cooperation on drug trafficking and expanded military-to-military contact were all raised as possible deliverables.

  • Trump and Xi strike a cordial tone at the summit amid underlying tensions
    NPR24 Sep

    The two leaders visibly set the friction aside for the day. Worth reading against the substance: nothing announced changes a tariff rate, and the achievement on offer is that a deadline moved rather than a dispute closed.

  • ‘Hostile, but hooked’: what’s behind the US-China trade truce extension
    Al Jazeera24 SepAnalysis

    The argument is in the title: each side has the means to hurt the other badly — rare earths one way, market access and chips the other — and neither can afford to use them. That is not a peace, it is a mutual hostage arrangement being renewed two months at a time.

AI 2

The industry asked the Security Council for rules in public, while the two governments that matter discussed them in private.

  • AI corporate leaders tell the UN the industry needs global regulation
    Al Jazeera24 Sep

    Amodei and Altman before the Security Council, asking for oversight; Michael Kratsios rejecting it on behalf of the United States in the same session. The sceptical reading — that a rulebook written to the frontier labs’ specification is a moat — has been made in this brief before and still applies. It coexists with the fact that they said it in public, on the record, and Washington refused them.

  • The US and China are quietly talking AI guardrails — even as Trump publicly rejects them
    Fortune24 Sep

    Including, reportedly, a hotline of the kind built for nuclear weapons. The gap between the public position and the working one is the story: “globalist scheme” at the rostrum, technical de-confliction in the room. That pattern is familiar from arms control, which is either reassuring or exactly the wrong precedent.
