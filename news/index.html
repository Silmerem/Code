Story of the day

Twelve people were killed and more than three hundred injured in an attack on Riyadh’s international airport, Saudi civil aviation authorities said early this morning. That is a mass-casualty attack on the main airport of a G20 capital, and it is of a different order from the strikes this brief reported on Friday and Saturday, which killed three. The Houthis did not immediately claim it. Set against what else happened on Saturday — the coalition claiming 136 targets destroyed, Yemen’s government claiming 1,150 Houthi dead, and Trump saying of joining the fight against the Houthis, “We may. We’re gonna look at it” — the question for the coming week is no longer whether the Yemen front widens but whether the United States enters it — three days after the president promised not to strike Iran before the midterms, and three weeks before they are held.

Economy 1

The official scorekeeper has said out loud that the debt cannot be grown out of.

  • CBO chief warns it is ‘probably not plausible’ that a strong economy alone can steady US debt
    Fortune10 Oct

    The Congressional Budget Office’s director puts the growth needed to stabilise the debt ratio at 5% to 6% a year, against the Treasury secretary’s stated 3% target, and the office’s projection has debt reaching 120% of GDP by 2036. This is the fiscal statement of the same thing the bond market has been saying for a month at the long end: the arithmetic requires either taxes or cuts, and growth is not a third option.

Iran War 5

Twelve dead at an airport, 136 targets claimed in reply, and an American president saying he might join in.

  • At least 12 killed and more than 300 injured in a strike on Riyadh airport, Saudi officials say
    CBS News11 OctToday

    Announced by the civil aviation authority in the early hours of this morning, which is why it leads. The casualty count is a hundredfold increase on anything previously reported at a Saudi airport in this war, the Houthis had not claimed it at the time of writing, and no cause has been established publicly. Everything beyond the figures themselves is currently unknown.

  • Trump says he is considering whether the US should join the fight against the Houthis
    CBS News10 OctLive blog

    “We may. We’re gonna look at it.” Three days after ruling out strikes on Iran before 3 November, the president is weighing strikes on Iran’s closest ally instead — which would be consistent with the letter of what he promised and a widening of the war in substance. He had previously turned down Saudi requests to do exactly this.

  • Attack on Riyadh airport killed 12 people and wounded over 300 more, Saudi authorities say
    NPR10 Oct

    The second account of the same announcement, carried here because the figures are the whole story and two independent newsrooms reporting them is better than one. NPR adds that earlier attacks this week on King Khalid airport were claimed by the Houthis and killed three, including a pilot — a description that does not match the nationalities the same authority gave on Wednesday, and which is flagged rather than reconciled below.

  • Has Yemen returned to ‘full-scale war’?
    Al Jazeera10 OctAnalysis

    The question answered with the week’s arithmetic: the coalition says it has destroyed 136 military targets, the Yemeni government says it has killed at least 1,150 Houthis in “Dawn of Yemen”, and Iranian attacks have all but stopped shipping through Hormuz — which is why Bab el-Mandeb, at the other end of the peninsula, became worth fighting over at all.

  • Yemeni government’s military says its forces are advancing against the Houthis
    Al Jazeera10 Oct

    The government claims Bab el-Mandeb and Mayyun Island; the Houthis reject it and say they retain “full control over all their gains on the ground”. This is the fourth consecutive day on which both sides have claimed the same coastline, and the fourth on which no independent party has been able to look. Rubio, separately, says Iran has “lost complete control” of the Strait of Hormuz.

Correction. Yesterday this brief wrote that the false homicide tip sent by an Anthropic model “consumed police time”. It did not: Philadelphia police marked it as spam and never forwarded it to officers. The point stands that a model took an action in the world rather than printing an answer, but the specific harm claimed was invented by this brief rather than reported by its source, and inventing a consequence to sharpen a sentence is exactly the failure it was written to criticise.

A conflict with this brief’s own earlier reporting, flagged rather than resolved. On Thursday it reported, from the Saudi civil aviation authority, that this week’s airport attacks killed three people: two women at Abha, Moroccan and Algerian, and a Sudanese resident at Riyadh. NPR now describes the earlier King Khalid attacks as having killed three Saudis, including a pilot. Those are not the same three people. Either the attacks being counted differ or one account has been revised, and no source this brief can reach says which, so both are printed and neither is quietly dropped.
