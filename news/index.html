Story of the day

The G7 is opening its emergency reserves: 100 million barrels of crude and diesel, starting immediately, running four months, with the diesel portion compressed into twenty days. Strategic stocks exist for a physical supply emergency, and after eight months of war that is now a defensible description of the fuel market — American diesel hit a record $6.50 a gallon on Friday, European diesel an all-time high of €2.24 a litre, and British diesel passed £2 a litre for the first time. What got Europe to move was a US threat to ban its own diesel exports, which Trump has now said out loud he was never going to carry out. The reserves are real and finite; the threat that unlocked them was not.

Finance 3

The largest coordinated release of emergency fuel in years, and an argument about how much of it is already gone.

  • G7 to release 100 million barrels of oil and diesel — will it curb prices?
    Al Jazeera3 Oct

    The mechanics: release begins at once and runs four months, with a concentrated diesel tranche over twenty days. The piece’s own answer to its headline is the honest one — some downward pressure, mostly on diesel, and short-lived, because a reserve release substitutes for supply it cannot replace. Nothing here reopens the Strait of Hormuz.

  • After threatening to ban US exports of diesel fuel, Trump admits ‘we were never going to do it’
    Fortune3 Oct

    The threat worked and was then disowned within days, which tells you something about how the next one will be read. The price backdrop is not rhetorical: $6.50 for a US gallon against $5.61 a month ago, and a European record of €2.24 a litre on Commission figures.

  • IEA says 325 million barrels of emergency oil released, with 75 million still to come
    Euronews3 Oct

    The number that puts the G7 announcement in proportion. These are different accountings — the IEA is totting up the whole war-period programme, the G7 figure is this week’s new tranche — and this brief is not going to add them together. Read side by side they say the same thing: the buffer is being spent faster than it can be rebuilt.

AI 2

Someone who wrote the safety reports has resigned and explained, in public, why he stopped believing them.

  • OpenAI safety employee resigns, claiming the company’s ‘culture is broken’
    TechCrunch3 Oct

    David Robinson spent three and a half years at OpenAI and led the writing of the safety reports that accompany its major launches, which makes his essay in The Atlantic unusually hard to dismiss. His argument is structural rather than moral: “iterative deployment” — ship, find the failures, patch them — guarantees periodic failures, and the failures scale with the systems. He wants frontier labs run like nuclear plants or busy airports, and says he never met a colleague at OpenAI who had done either.

  • The GPT Oracle: one in ten Americans are reviving the pagan practice of prophecy
    Fortune3 OctAnalysis

    The hook is overcooked; the finding underneath is not. Pew puts the share of Americans using chatbots for emotional support or companionship at 10%, and the piece’s point is about opacity — people are consulting a system whose reasoning they cannot inspect, and treating the confidence of the answer as evidence for it. That is a usage statistic about the week’s other story, not a separate topic.

The Register’s report on OpenAI dismissing three safety researchers, and Axios on the wider researcher revolt, are both dated 2 October and so fall outside the window; they ran as background to the item above rather than as entries.

UK Politics 2

Two policing decisions about who may protest where, four days before a date that makes both harder.

  • Met calls on students to move pro-Palestine march on the 7 October anniversary
    ITV News3 Oct

    A request rather than a ban, which is the distinction that will be argued over all week. The Met has powers to impose conditions on a march’s route and timing under public order law, and asking first is what a force does when it would rather not test them on an anniversary that is itself the reason for the objection.

  • Heavy police presence as anti-migrant protests take place in Southampton and Dover
    ITV News3 Oct

    Hundreds turned out in two port towns, with restrictions in place and warnings issued about face coverings. The port setting is the argument in physical form, and these have been recurring rather than one-off — the policing question is becoming a standing commitment rather than an operation.

Both from ITV News, which is the per-section limit for one outlet, and both dated yesterday, so neither needs the est. marker the looser UK rules allow. Westminster itself filed nothing: the Commons is not sitting on a Saturday and the Budget is still three and a half weeks away.

Iran War 4

The war’s centre of gravity moved to Yemen this weekend, and the casualty claims are all coming from the people making them.

  • Iran war live: Yemeni forces strike Sanaa as Trump warns Tehran of the ‘hard way’
    Al Jazeera4 OctTodayLive blog

    This morning’s rolling page, and the only entry in this edition filed today. The strikes on the Houthi-held capital are continuing into a second day while the president’s public line hardens again — the pattern of this war has been that the rhetoric sharpens while the talks carry on underneath it, and neither has yet overruled the other.

  • Yemen’s army says it targeted Houthis in hundreds of strikes, killing 700
    Al Jazeera3 Oct

    The internationally recognised government’s spokesman claims 257 “precision targeting operations” in a day using aircraft, drones, artillery and rockets, more than 1,500 Houthi casualties and at least 700 killed. Every one of those numbers is the attacking army’s own estimate of its own work, released the same day, with no independent count available — which is how casualty figures from this front have arrived all week.

  • Yemen government forces say they struck Houthi targets
    France 243 Oct

    Worth reading against the item above for the disagreement about who is doing the bombing: the Yemeni government claims the strikes on Sanaa as its own, while the Houthis attribute dozens of them to Saudi Arabia. That is not a detail. Whether this is a civil war with a backer or a Saudi air campaign with a local front determines what the war is and who is a party to it.

  • Senior Trump administration officials met to discuss the Iran war at Camp David
    CBS News3 OctLive blog

    Vance, Rubio, Hegseth, Witkoff, Ratcliffe and the chairman of the Joint Chiefs met on Friday on both the Iran war and the Yemen escalation. Trump’s line afterwards was that Iran is “negotiating on fumes” and wants a deal, which is the same assessment he offered before rejecting the last proposal. The attendance list is the information here: this was the war cabinet, not an update.

The CBS entry is the same rolling live blog this brief linked yesterday — its address has no date and does not change, though its contents have. It is flagged here rather than quietly re-run.
