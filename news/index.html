Story of the day

The Federal Reserve raised interest rates for the first time in more than three years, a quarter point to a target range of 3.75–4%, on a 12–0 vote, and signalled more to come: sixteen of the eighteen officials who submit projections expect another rise this year, four of them two. Kevin Warsh’s justification was blunt — inflation has been “too high… for too long”, and “this summer’s inflation readings do not tell me that underlying trends have meaningfully improved”. The bond market’s answer was to push the ten-year yield straight back to 5%. This is a central bank tightening into a price shock made by a war, and the market is not treating one rise as the end of it.

Finance 4

A unanimous hike, a chairman who would not say it was the last one, and a yield curve that believed him.

  • Fed raises rates to 3.75–4% and signals another increase is possible this year
    CNBC16 Sep

    Twelve votes to nil, a quarter point, and a dot plot in which sixteen of eighteen participants pencil in a further rise. Warsh has chosen not to submit a dot since taking the chair, which removes the single most-watched marker from the chart and leaves the committee’s median to speak for a chairman who has not joined it.

  • What Kevin Warsh said about the Fed’s first rate hike since 2023
    CNN16 Sep

    “We must be confident that underlying inflation is moving to our objective clearly and at sufficient speed.” The word carrying the weight is underlying: headline inflation is being driven by crude, which the Fed cannot touch, so the case for moving rests on stopping an energy shock from becoming a wage-and-price expectation.

  • Ten-year Treasury yield climbs back to 5% after the Fed hikes and Warsh highlights inflation risks
    CNBC16 Sep

    A rate rise that convinces the bond market usually pulls long yields down, on the logic that inflation will be contained. This one did the opposite. That is the market saying the problem is bigger than the response, or that the borrowing and issuance behind the supply of bonds matters more than the policy rate.

  • The Fed raises interest rates for the first time in over three years
    NPR16 Sep

    The plain account, and the one worth reading for scale: an entire cutting cycle has now been reversed by a conflict in the Gulf. Everything the committee did between 2023 and this summer was premised on inflation continuing to fall, and that premise has been withdrawn by the oil price.

Economy 2

The same shock, arriving in Britain a month behind and through the same pump.

  • UK inflation jumps to 3.1% as energy costs soar
    CNBC16 Sep

    Up from 2.9% in July and the first reading above 3% since March, with motor fuel 23% dearer than a year ago doing most of the work. The Bank of England decides today and is expected to hold at 3.75% — which would leave Britain the outlier among the three central banks this brief has covered this month, after the ECB moved on 10 September and the Fed yesterday.

    The running account of the decision and the reaction across the afternoon, including the market reversals either side of the press conference. Useful for seeing which specific sentence moved what, which the tidy write-ups necessarily lose.

AI 2

The industry’s agreement to slow down lasted about four days.

  • Zuckerberg says AI doesn’t need an industry-wide slowdown because competition will make models safe
    Fortune16 Sep

    Meta’s position against the pact reported on Tuesday, with Jensen Huang on the same side and Dario Amodei on the other — the two of them speaking at Salesforce’s Dreamforce on the same day. The argument that competition produces safety is the one the labs themselves abandoned last weekend, and it is now being made by the companies that did not sign up.

  • Robots are waiting for a ChatGPT moment. Nvidia’s Les Karpas explains why
    TechCrunch16 Sep

    The bottleneck is data, not models: there is no internet-scale corpus of physical interaction the way there was of text. It is a useful corrective to the week’s other AI coverage — the capability that frightens people in language has no equivalent in the physical world yet, for a reason nobody has solved.

UK Politics 1

A prime minister who campaigned on ending austerity is preparing the ground for a Budget he has to pay for.

  • British PM says he will make ‘difficult decisions’ as inflation rises
    France 2416 Sep

    Burnham’s response to the 3.1% print, and the phrase is the one every chancellor reaches for before a tax rise. The Budget is on 28 October, gilt yields are at multi-decade highs and the fuel component of inflation is set by a war — a combination that leaves a government elected on public investment very little room to fund it.

One headline, and not from a British outlet. ITV News — the only reachable UK newsroom that dates its URLs — filed no Westminster story on 16 September, so this comes from France 24’s wire service, which does. That is the structural gap in this section stated plainly: British politics is covered here through whoever else was in the room.

Iran War 1

Washington says days. The satellite images suggest months. The oil price has picked a side.

  • Oil prices fall after the US says the damaged Saudi pipeline will restart in days
    CNBC16 Sep

    The market took the American estimate and sold. Set against it, in the same reporting: satellite imagery appears to show extensive damage to one of the East-West line’s pumping stations, and at least one analyst puts the repair at months rather than days. Both claims cannot be right, and the gap between them is worth several dollars a barrel.

One headline. The day’s Gulf news was the pipeline and the oil price; Al Jazeera, CNN, NPR, France 24 and Euronews returned nothing else dated 16 or 17 September on the fighting itself.
