Story of the day

Wednesday’s intervention lasted one day. Treasury yields climbed back above where they stood before the buyback was announced — the 10-year to 4.704%, the 30-year to 5.248% — and equities took the news badly, the Dow shedding 704 points. Meanwhile the Treasury secretary who authorised the buyback spent Thursday promising to “collapse” Iran with economic pressure. The same man is trying to talk down the cost of American borrowing and talk up a campaign that keeps oil above $92; the bond market appears to have noticed.

Finance 2

The relief rally gave everything back and then some, which is the market’s answer to Wednesday.

  • A steep sell-off sparked by a move back higher in Treasury yields
    CNBC20 AugLive updates

    The Dow lost 703.84 points, or 1.32%, to 52,759.21; the S&P fell 0.87% to 7,641.16 and the Nasdaq 1%. Wednesday’s gains are gone and then some. The worry named in the piece is the plain one — that borrowing costs at these levels end the bull market regardless of what earnings do.

  • Why the bond market is acting like it did before the Great Recession
    Fortune20 Aug

    Worth reading for the comparison rather than the prediction. The 30-year is back above 5%, its 2007 level, and the piece sets out what is genuinely similar — term premium rebuilding, duration supply overwhelming demand — and what is not. A parallel drawn to 2007 is a strong claim; this one at least shows its working.

Two lines, not three: Thursday’s market news was the bond move, reported in Economy below, and the oil move, which belongs with the war that caused it.

Economy 2

The buyback was reversed by the market in a day, and the Fed minutes explain why nobody believes rates are coming down.

  • Treasury yields rebound, wiping out the decline that followed Bessent's intervention
    CNBC20 Aug

    The 10-year rose more than five basis points to 4.704% — above where it sat before Wednesday’s announcement — and the 30-year more than five to 5.248%. Bessent said the operations could run larger than the $4bn floor, starting 9 September. Strategists quoted make the same point twice over: a buyback changes who holds the debt, not how much there is.

  • Treasury rushes into the bond market as Fed minutes show many governors want to hike, not cut
    Fortune20 Aug

    This is the collision. The Treasury is buying long paper to push yields down while the central bank’s own record shows a committee leaning towards raising rates. Two arms of the same government pulling opposite ways is not a policy mix, and the bond market is pricing the contradiction rather than either side of it.

UK Politics 2

A crackdown launched from a Bradford tip, and a question about the Sussexes that Downing Street declined to answer.

  • Harry and Meghan's UK security arrangements 'a private matter', says Burnham
    ITV News20 Aug

    The couple return to Britain later this month, to a non-royal residence outside London, after six years away; the King was told three days before it became public. The prime minister would not say who pays for their protection, wishing them “well in the moves they’re making”. “A private matter” is a holding line on a question that is about public money.

  • Burnham plans a crackdown on gangs profiting from illegal waste
    ITV News19 Aug

Three more “toxic dumps” to be cleared. It is the fly-tipping strand of the summer tour arriving as enforcement rather than a promise.

Iran War 5

The war changed instrument on Thursday: from ships and missiles to banks, airports and anyone who trades with Tehran.

  • US imposes new sanctions on Hezbollah and threatens economic pressure on Iran
    CNN20 AugLive blog

    Thursday’s thread. Trump had promised on Wednesday, in capitals, “the MOST CRUSHING ECONOMIC OPERATION EVER TAKEN AGAINST ANY COUNTRY”; Thursday brought the first concrete piece of it. Iran’s foreign ministry called the threat unlawful and the Revolutionary Guard said it could field more “destructive” weapons if fighting resumes.

  • Analysis: Trump has a new plan to crush Iran's economy. But will he stay the course?
    CNN20 AugAnalysis

    The question the announcements avoid. Sanctions campaigns work on a timescale of years and this administration has changed course on Iran repeatedly since February. The piece also notes what the pivot to economics is quietly conceding: the military option has been expensive, unpopular, and has not reopened the strait.

  • US Treasury secretary says new economic measures will 'collapse' Iran
    Al Jazeera20 Aug

    Bessent’s framing, and the significant caveat inside it: “If we are doing the maximum economic pressure, then that means that likely there will not be a large-scale kinetic restart.” Read plainly, the Treasury secretary is telling the markets the shooting phase is over — which is a considerably bigger statement than the sanctions themselves.

  • Trump pledges 'economic warfare' on Iran, prompting Tehran derision
    France 2420 Aug

    Tehran’s answer was mockery rather than alarm, which is itself information: after seven months of war and a closed strait, there is not much left to sanction that has not been sanctioned. The threat with real teeth is the secondary one — consequences for any country whose banks, businesses or airports give Iran a “lifeline”.

  • Oil prices rise after the Treasury secretary says the US will collapse Iran with economic pressure
    CNBC20 Aug

    Brent for October reached $92.09 a barrel and WTI for September $86.07. This is the loop the whole brief keeps returning to: pressure on Iran lifts crude, crude lifts inflation expectations, inflation expectations lift the yields the Treasury spent Wednesday trying to push down.
