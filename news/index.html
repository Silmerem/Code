Story of the day

France has become a bond story. Shares sold off across Europe and Asia on Monday as unrest in France drove the ten-year yield to its highest since 2002 — up about eight basis points to 4.94%, with the gap over Germany back to roughly 148 points and the CAC down 1.1%. The estimated cost of the riot damage has doubled, and the pressure is now on the president personally. This brief has spent three weeks reporting a sovereign debt repricing driven by war and energy. Monday added the other kind of risk: a government that may not be able to hold its streets, in the second largest economy of a currency union, priced accordingly within a single session.

Economy 2

A weak American housing number, and a bill for the damage in France that nobody has agreed who pays.

  • 10-year Treasury yield moves lower after weak housing data
    CNBC20 Oct

    September housing starts came in below expectations and the long end fell on it — the first American data in a fortnight to point at weakness rather than at prices. Housing is the sector that responds to interest rates first and most, and long rates have been at multi-decade highs for a month even though the Fed has raised only once. The mortgage market does not wait for the central bank.

  • France’s riot damage doubles: who will pay for thousands of wrecked shops?
    Euronews20 Oct

    The estimate of the destruction has doubled and the question of who covers it is unresolved between insurers, the state and the shopkeepers. This is the part of a political crisis that reaches a bond market: not the protests but the fiscal commitment that follows them, arriving in a country whose borrowing costs are already at a 24-year high.

    CNBC20 OctLive updates

    Disclosed alongside results showing SpaceX renting AI computing capacity to Google, Anthropic and others, reported talks to raise up to $40 billion for more Nvidia chips, and a target of volume AI-chip production with Tesla and Intel by late 2028. A launch company that sells compute and intends to make processors is not a launch company, and the merger committees are the governance catching up with that.

  • German chancellor urges businesses to embrace AI with Nvidia support
    CNBC20 Oct

    A Berlin summit, investments in four German startups, and a European commitment from Nvidia put at close to $10 billion with no individual sums disclosed. The pattern is now familiar from London and Brussels this month: the supplier tells a government it is falling behind, and the government announces a partnership with the supplier.
