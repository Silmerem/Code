Economy 3

Three tankers alight, a blockade extended to land, and a pay offer in Dublin. The first two are here rather than in the war section because they are the supply side of every energy price on this page.

  • Iran says three tankers caught fire in the Strait of Hormuz
    Al Arabiya8 Nov

    Three in one day, after the one that struck a mine on Saturday, in a strait where at least eleven ships were attacked in the week to Friday and traffic is running about 65% below pre-war levels. The Revolutionary Guard says it targets vessels transiting without its authorisation. A chokepoint does not need to be closed to stop working; it only needs to be uninsurable.

  • US launches a task force to disrupt Iran’s land-based supply routes
    Al Jazeera8 Nov

    Central Command extending a months-old naval blockade onto land. The same report notes Iraq saying it has reached understandings with Washington to move its own oil exports through a lane near Oman — so the squeeze tightens on Iran while a neighbour is quietly let through. Blockades are always also a licensing system.

  • Government makes a ‘substantially improved’ pay offer to public sector unions
    ITV News8 Nov

    This is the Irish government, not the British one — worth saying plainly, because the headline does not. Talks adjourned on Sunday without agreement and resume today, with a twenty-four-hour strike set for Thursday, during Ireland’s presidency of the EU Council. A state improving an offer and still facing a walkout is the shape of every public-sector negotiation in a year of 4% inflation.

Iran War 4

A missile reached Riyadh’s financial district, and the accounts of what it did diverge.

  • Building owned by Saudi oil giant damaged in missile attack on Riyadh’s financial district
    CNN8 Nov

    Saudi authorities describe an attempted attack with minimal impact; CNN says it verified video showing windows blown out across several floors of an Aramco building. Both statements can stand — a tower with broken glass is not a destroyed tower — but the gap between “attempted” and “several floors” is where the next insurance premium on the Riyadh skyline gets set.

  • Yemen’s Houthis claim an attack on an Aramco skyscraper in Riyadh
    France 248 Nov

    The claim itself, from the group making it. Hitting the financial district rather than an airport or a refinery is a change of target class: the previous month’s strikes aimed at where oil is moved, and this one aimed at where it is sold. The intended audience is not the Saudi air force.

  • Saudi Aramco building in Riyadh damaged in an ‘attempted’ attack
    Al Jazeera8 Nov

    The same day’s interceptions over Riyadh and the Eastern Province, and the other half of the ledger: Houthi-run health officials say thirty-three people including eight women and thirteen children were killed in coalition air attacks on the village of al-Shariyah near Sanaa. That figure comes from one side’s administration about the other side’s aircraft and is not verified here. It is carried because leaving it out would make this a report of one country’s casualties.

  • Saudi-led coalition intercepts five missiles targeting Riyadh and other Saudi regions
    Al Arabiya8 Nov

    Five in a day, which is the number that matters more than any single impact: a defence that works ninety per cent of the time against a barrage this size still lets something through most weeks. The coalition’s own count is the source, and it has an interest in both halves of it — the interceptions and the barrage.
