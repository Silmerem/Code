Story of the day — and a follow-up to yesterday’s labelled claim

Yesterday this brief carried, and labelled, a single-source claim that the Houthis had struck a market in Taiz with a ballistic missile. A day later the picture is worse and less clear: government forces say a Houthi missile hit a market at al-Waziiya in Taiz governorate, killing and wounding dozens, while the Houthis blame Saudi air strikes for a market attack in Taiz and the government rejects that. France 24 reports thirty dead. Two sides of a war are now accusing each other of destroying a marketplace. Someone did it, both accounts cannot be right, and nothing available to this brief can tell you which. That is the honest state of the most serious story of the week, and labelling it is the most I can do with it.

Finance 2

The money leaving AI went to the one company that never joined it.

  • Berkshire shares surge as Wall Street swoons amid AI fears
    CNBC7 Nov

    The Nasdaq finished the week down 3.4% and the S&P 500 about 2.7%, with the data-centre builders at the centre of the selling again, while Berkshire rose. A conglomerate of railways, insurers and utilities being the week’s refuge is the market saying it would like to own something that already earns money.

  • Berkshire Hathaway stock is a haven again as investors flee the AI bloodbath — while its own portfolio takes a hit
    Fortune7 Nov

    The qualification the first story needs: Berkshire is up 13.6% this year against 7.8% for the S&P 500, and its own holdings were hurt in the reshuffle. A haven that is also an investor is not insulated from what it is sheltering people from, which is worth knowing before anyone treats a share price as a bunker.

Economy 2

What the new Congress means for power generation, and who is starting businesses.

  • What the midterm blue wave rebuke of Trump means for renewable energy
    CNBC7 Nov

    The other half of the data-centre story, and the half with the longer consequences. The buildout’s constraint is electricity; the new Congress and the new governors are more disposed to renewables and less disposed to the centres that would consume them. Those two preferences are in tension and the week’s coverage has mostly reported only one of them.

  • America’s newest small business owners with no employees are off to a hot start in the AI era
    CNBC7 Nov

    Business applications from owners with no employees up 26.8% between early 2024 and early 2026, concentrated in AI-exposed industries. Read it two ways and both are in the data: people building something with cheaper tools, and people who used to be employees now invoicing instead. This is the figure that will be used to argue both sides of the jobs question for the next decade.

Iran War 2

Eleven ships attacked in a week, and a diplomatic effort running at twelve foreign ministers in forty-eight hours.

  • Yemeni government forces report advances as the Houthis say deadly strikes hit Sanaa
    Al Jazeera7 Nov

    The Taiz market claims set out in the lede above, alongside a Houthi account of at least 27 killed by strikes on a village in Sanaa province and government claims of repelling assaults in Marib. Every figure in this paragraph comes from a belligerent about its enemy. The one thing all of them agree on is that the ground war has intensified in the week the diplomacy intensified too.

  • Iran’s navy says a tanker hit a mine and caught fire in Hormuz
    Al Arabiya7 Nov

    One more vessel in a strait where the UK Maritime Trade Operations centre counted at least eleven ships attacked in the week to Friday, with transits running about 65% below pre-conflict levels. Iran’s foreign minister says he spoke to twelve foreign ministers in forty-eight hours, including Oman, Qatar, Saudi Arabia and Pakistan; Washington has moved to block Iran’s land supply routes as well as its ports. Mines do not distinguish between the ships of states that are negotiating and states that are not.
