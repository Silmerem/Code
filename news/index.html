Story of the day

One company added about $440bn of market value in a single session — more than the entire market capitalisation of all but a handful of firms on earth — and carried the whole index up with it. Nvidia rose 8.7%, the Nasdaq 1.57%, and it was the best day for tech since 4 August. Jensen Huang’s line was the one that did it: demand is “much greater than 70%”, and the only limit is how much he can build. That is a bullish statement and a fragile one, because it means the number everyone is now modelling is a supply forecast rather than a demand one.

Finance 2

The best day for the S&P and the Nasdaq in more than three weeks, and one stock did most of it.

  • Nasdaq jumps 1.57% for its best session since early August
    CNBC27 AugLive updates

    The S&P rose 0.72% to 7,730.99 and the Dow 105 points to 53,569.44 — a much smaller move, which tells you where the gain came from. The semiconductor complex followed Nvidia up: Broadcom 4.5%, Intel 4%, SK Hynix 2%.

  • AI giant Nvidia's stock jumps after strong second-quarter earnings
    The Hill27 Aug

    Shares closed above $227, up about 8.5%, on quarterly revenue of $96.2bn — 106% higher than a year ago. The forecast that moved it came from finance chief Colette Kress rather than Huang: 70% revenue growth next fiscal year, against the 44% analysts had modelled.

Economy 2

A quiet Thursday in the bond market, because everything is waiting for one speech this morning.

  • Treasury yields are little changed ahead of Jackson Hole
    CNBC27 Aug

    The 10-year at 4.672%, the 30-year at 5.19%, the 2-year at 4.232%, none of them moving as much as a basis point. Jobless claims came in at 203,000 against 208,000 expected — still historically low, and one more reason the Fed has no cover to cut.

  • The stakes are high as Kevin Warsh gives his first major speech as Fed chair
    NPR27 Aug

    His keynote is this morning — the first since he took office in May, and 19 days before a rate decision. He speaks into a bond market where the Treasury secretary has spent a fortnight doing what central banks normally do. Deutsche Bank’s Matthew Luzzetti adds the complication: “in the near term at least, there’s clear evidence that AI is an inflationary force”.

AI 4

The biggest day the sector has had in months, and three quieter stories that all point at its edges.

  • Nvidia adds more than $400 billion in value after blowout earnings boost AI confidence
    CNBC27 Aug

    About $440bn in one session. For scale: that is roughly the market value Nvidia created on Thursday afternoon, against a US federal deficit heading for $2tn across the whole year. The AI trade is now large enough that its good days are macroeconomic events.

  • What we learned from Nvidia's big day
    Axios27 Aug

    The number to keep from this one: seven big technology companies have committed roughly $3tn to AI infrastructure in off-balance-sheet arrangements. Off-balance-sheet is the phrase to sit with. Nvidia’s results settle whether the demand is real this quarter; they say nothing about what those commitments look like in a bad one.

  • Chinese open-source AI is starting to win over US businesses
    Fortune27 Aug

    Spending data rather than sentiment: the share of AI-buying businesses paying for model-serving platforms — the route to open-source and Chinese models — rose to 6.1% in July from 4.5% in January. Small, and moving in one direction, against frontier labs whose valuations assume it does not.

  • Anthropic makes its first move into physical AI with a universal standard
    Fortune27 Aug

    A Model Hardware Standard, built with the HHMI Janelia Research Campus, meant to let companies wire models into laboratory and factory equipment in “hours or minutes” rather than weeks. Worth watching for a reason unrelated to Anthropic: the productivity gain nine executives in ten could not find last week is supposed to live in exactly this kind of plumbing.

Iran War 3

Qatar joins Oman at the table, and a tanker is attacked in the strait Washington says is open.

  • Qatari PM heads to Iran to 'de-escalate tensions' as a tanker attack tests Trump's Hormuz claim
    CNBC27 Aug

    The contradiction in one line: the president has said the strait is “open and operating”, and a vessel was attacked in it this week. Meanwhile the Revolutionary Guards say Iran and Oman have agreed each country’s share of the waters — and of the revenue from administering them. That is a settlement being negotiated over Washington’s head.

  • Iran and Qatar hold Hormuz talks amid international hopes that dialogue with the US will resume
    Al Jazeera27 Aug

    Doha and Muscat brokered the June memorandum that expired on 17 August; both are now back, without the United States in the room. Tehran’s conditions have not moved since June — blockade lifted, assets unfrozen, oil sanctions removed — which is why the talks that are happening are about administering the strait rather than reopening it.

  • Iran and Qatar hold Hormuz talks as the US blockade squeezes Iranian oil exports
    France 2427 Aug

    The half of the campaign that is working. Whatever the sanctions announcement did or did not contain, the naval blockade is measurably cutting Iranian exports — which is also the strongest argument that Tehran has for refusing to reopen the strait until it is lifted.
