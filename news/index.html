Story of the day

The Yemen war is now killing civilians inside Saudi airports. Missiles and drones hit Abha International on Tuesday and Riyadh’s King Khalid International on Wednesday; the civil aviation authority counts three dead and thirty-six injured, and all three of the dead were foreign residents — a Moroccan woman and an Algerian woman at Abha, a Sudanese man at Riyadh. One missile came down near the Riyadh runway minutes before a flight from Cairo was due; it was diverted to Jeddah. The coalition says the attacks “will not go unpunished” and that it has struck eighty-two targets in reply. Six days ago this was a frozen civil war that had restarted on a coastline. It is now an exchange of fire over two capitals, and the people it has killed this week were migrant workers waiting for planes.

Finance 2

The Fed has told us there is one more rise coming and declined to say when.

  • Fed officials see another hike coming, but no sign as to when, minutes show
    CNBC7 Oct

    September’s minutes: most participants thought a further increase would likely be appropriate by year end, with none expected in 2027 — one more and then stop. The committee describes the labour market as close to maximum employment, which is a striking thing to write about a month that produced 29,000 jobs. Two meetings remain, on 28 October and 9 December, from a target range of 3.75% to 4%.

  • 10-year Treasury yield backs off from a 24-year high after a solid bond auction eases demand fears
    CNBC7 Oct

    The 10-year went above 5.35% intraday, the highest since April 2002, and the 30-year to 5.732%, the highest since May 2002 — then both retreated once the Treasury had sold $39 billion of 10-year notes without difficulty. That is the real test each week now: not what the Fed intends but whether anyone turns up to buy the paper.

Economy 1

The public now expects more inflation than at any time since the last peak.

  • Inflation fears on the rise as the one-year outlook in a Fed survey hits its highest level since May 2023
    CNBC7 Oct

    The New York Fed’s survey puts the median expectation for prices over the next twelve months at 3.9%. Expectations are the one inflation input a central bank treats as partly self-fulfilling, because people who expect higher prices ask for higher wages and accept higher ones — which is why this number, published the same day as the minutes, makes the December rise more likely rather than less.

One headline. No statistical release of substance appeared yesterday beyond the survey above and the minutes in Finance, and the diesel and eurozone stories that would sit here ran earlier in the week.

AI 4

Two regulators and two companies all reached for the same instrument yesterday: independent verification.

  • Singapore’s central bank wants all fintech AI use cases subject to independent review
    The Register8 OctToday

    The Monetary Authority of Singapore’s first guidelines on AI risk management for financial institutions, published this morning, want every use case reviewed by someone independent before it reaches production. They do not bind until 7 October 2027, which is a year to build the review capacity that does not currently exist — and the shortage of people qualified to do that reviewing is the quiet constraint on every rule of this kind.

  • Anthropic reconfigures its security programme
    The Register7 Oct

    Project Glasswing and the Cyber Verification Programme merged into one offering with three access tiers, on the back of a claim that partners found at least 129,000 verified software vulnerabilities between April and July. The scepticism is specific and worth the weight: VulnCheck’s Patrick Garrity tracked 225 Anthropic-linked vulnerabilities and found fewer than 0.5% being exploited in the wild, which is an argument about whether a vulnerability found at machine scale is the same object as a vulnerability that matters. See the disclosure below.

  • Google’s new SynthID website can identify AI-generated media
    TechCrunch7 Oct

    A public page where anyone can check whether an image, video or audio clip carries Google’s generation marker. Two days after OpenAI said it would watermark text in the EU, so the industry is converging on marking its own output — but the marks only prove what carries them. A file with no marker is not thereby human, and testing on text watermarks has shown they weaken under ordinary editing.

  • AI coalition launches effort to pool computing power and reduce the supply crunch
    Axios7 Oct

    A National Compute Grid, pooling capacity against the shortage that is driving the infrastructure spending now visible in bond yields — the Fed itself named the AI build-out yesterday as one reason long rates are where they are. Shared grids are how electricity and telecoms matured; they are also how a handful of operators came to control both.

UK Politics 3

  • Badenoch vows to slash inheritance tax in a ‘battle for the soul of the nation’
    ITV News7 Oct

    No inheritance tax on the family home, and an extra £1 million transferable between couples, which she says would halve the number of estates paying it. Note the change between Tuesday’s briefing and Wednesday’s speech: the trail said she would cut the 40% rate above the £325,000 threshold, which is a different and more expensive policy than exempting a class of asset. The second version is the one she delivered.

  • Badenoch takes a swipe at Reform UK as the Tory leader issues an election battlecry
    GB News7 Oct

With her party behind Labour and narrowly behind Reform, ruling out the arrangement that the arithmetic suggests is the central bet of her leadership.

Iran War 4

Two airports hit, eighty-two targets struck in reply, and no independent count of any of it.

  • Saudi Arabia strikes back after deadly Houthi attacks on Riyadh and airports
    France 248 OctToday

    The retaliation, filed this morning: eighty-two targets claimed, including mountain-based ballistic missile facilities, command and control, communications and assembly points, plus the destruction of the launch platform used against Riyadh. The coalition’s phrase is that the attacks “will not go unpunished”, which is the language of a campaign rather than of a response.

  • Three killed, 36 injured in attacks on Saudi airports
    Al Arabiya7 Oct

    The civil aviation authority’s own account, and the most precise numbers available: two women killed at Abha, Moroccan and Algerian, with twenty-eight injured; one Sudanese resident killed at Riyadh with eight injured; material damage unquantified. Both airports activated emergency procedures. Every one of the dead was a foreign resident of the kingdom.

  • Yemen’s Houthis attack Aden airport; Saudi forces down a missile near Riyadh
    Al Jazeera7 Oct

    The same day from the other direction, and the detail that the targets include Aden — the government’s own seat — as well as Saudi territory. The Houthi spokesman claims accurate hits on Abha airport and the air base at Khamis Mushait, and the group separately says it has killed or wounded more than two hundred Saudi-backed fighters, a scale it calls unprecedented for itself.

  • Deadly Houthi attacks on Saudi airports highlight the widening fallout from Yemen’s civil war
    CNN7 OctAnalysis

    The widening is the point. A war that was about a coastline a week ago is now closing airspace over the Arabian peninsula, and the states supplying the government side — with Türkiye and Pakistan now committed to deploying forces to the kingdom under a mutual defence pact — have more exposure each week. Civil aviation is the first thing to go in a conflict like this and the last to come back.
