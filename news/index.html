Story of the day

The president has waived the Russia sanctions he signed into law himself. The package — duties on Russian goods and penalties for buying Russian oil and gas — was called off by executive order days before it took effect, and Congress, which passed it, is in recess until after the midterms and cannot do anything about it until November. The market read it as supply: Brent fell 1.8% to $107.88 and American crude to $100.25. Set it beside the other fact in this edition — Russia’s foreign minister arrives in Tehran today — and the shape of the week is a United States easing pressure on Moscow while fighting Moscow’s partner, and a Russian foreign minister in the capital of the country it is fighting.

Economy 1

A sanctions law passed by Congress has been suspended by the president who signed it, with Congress away.

  • Trump waives Russia sanctions. Where does that leave Congress?
    NPR18 OctAnalysis

    The measures were enacted in September with large majorities and would have hit importers of Russian crude and gas; the waiver suspends them on national-interest grounds until the administration decides otherwise. Members of both parties objected over the weekend, and the chamber does not sit again until after 3 November. A legislature that cannot meet cannot override, which is the part of this that will outlast the particular sanctions.

AI 2

The company that writes this brief is putting a twentieth of itself into a fund for the people its products displace.

  • Anthropic says it is committing 5% of its shares to a ‘public benefit fund’
    TechCrunch18 Oct

    Subject to shareholder approval, with the board able to add more later. At the valuation being discussed for next month’s listing, 5% is a very large number; at any valuation, a commitment that still needs a shareholder vote is a proposal rather than a transfer. The timing is a month before an IPO, which is when a company’s stated intentions are worth the most to it and cost the least.

  • Anthropic commits 5% of shares to a new public benefit fund that could address AI job disruption
    CNBC18 Oct

    The stated uses are charitable donations, direct payments to people, or programmes aimed at the effect of AI on work — three very different things, and no choice between them yet. A company setting aside equity against the harm its own product may cause is either unusually serious or unusually good at anticipating an argument, and nothing published so far settles which. See the disclosure below.

Iran War 2

Tehran says the strait can reopen in days, and Moscow’s foreign minister is on his way there.

  • Iran says the Strait of Hormuz reopening and seven-day ceasefire conditions are still on the table
    CBS News18 OctLive blog

    Araghchi said on Sunday the strait could reopen within days if Washington meets the conditions set out in September — ending the naval blockade, lifting oil sanctions, releasing frozen assets, a regional halt to hostilities. Trump rejected that package on 26 September and it has not changed since. An offer restated unchanged after three weeks of attacks on shipping is a negotiating position, not a concession. The same page carries Saudi strikes on Yemen that Houthi media say killed at least twelve.

  • Russia’s Lavrov to visit Tehran on Monday, Iran says
    Al Arabiya18 Oct

    Announced by Tehran, two days after Washington suspended the sanctions it had just imposed on Russia. Moscow and Tehran signed a twenty-year partnership treaty in January 2025 that stopped short of mutual defence. A visit at this moment is not obviously about Yemen or about Hormuz; it is about what Russia is now able to offer Iran that it could not offer a fortnight ago.
