Story of the day

Central Command began striking Revolutionary Guard targets inside Iran at noon Eastern on Tuesday — air defence, communications and radar sites — after two tankers were attacked in the Strait of Hormuz and, Washington says, an Iranian attempt to lay fresh sea mines. Trump called the strikes “large and powerful”. This is the fourth exchange in four days, and the first in which the United States has hit Iranian territory in a planned wave rather than a single retaliation. Brent closed 4.5% higher at $94.52.

A third losing day, and this time both halves of the trade went the wrong way at once.

  • Dow falls more than 400 points as surging yields and oil drag Wall Street lower
    CNBC1 SepLive updates

    The Nasdaq shed about 1% and technology led the fall, with the S&P down 0.7%. Three consecutive losing sessions to open September, after a month that finished up. What changed is not the war alone but the combination: an oil shock that raises inflation into a Fed already leaning towards a rise.

  • Oil is back above $92 — and US bond yields just hit their highest since January 2025
    Fortune1 Sep

    The 10-year at 4.79%, up from 4.73% on Friday, and the two-year at 4.35% against roughly 3.50% in January. Both moves point the same way, and neither is about growth. When the safe asset sells off on the same day as an equity rout, the thing being priced is inflation, not risk.

Economy 2

A global bond sell-off, and a labour market quietly weakening underneath it.

  • The bond market is in open revolt as Trump declines an Iranian peace deal and oil hits $92
    Fortune1 Sep

    This is not a US story any more. Japan’s 10-year touched 3%, a level unseen since 1996; Germany’s reached 3%, a 15-year high. Governments everywhere are being asked to pay more to borrow because of a war in a strait, which is the clearest measure yet of how far this conflict has travelled from the Gulf.

  • Treasury yields move higher as Middle East tensions return to focus
    CNBC1 Sep

    Tuesday’s data went almost unnoticed and should not have. ISM manufacturing came in at 54.6 against 55.3 expected, with its prices gauge stuck at 71.1; July job openings held at 7.27m but hiring fell by 278,000, taking the hiring rate to 3.2%, the lowest since February. Firms are not firing. They have stopped hiring.

Fed governor Michael Barr said he would back a rise “if inflation appears not to be moderating sufficiently”, echoing Warsh. Friday’s August payrolls now carry the whole decision.

Parliament came back, and so did the prime minister — for the first time in the Commons since he took the job.

  • UK PM Andy Burnham faces MPs for the first time since taking office
    France 241 SepLive blog

    Six weeks after entering Downing Street, and after a summer spent touring rather than legislating. Iran War 5

Tankers hit, a drone downed, and then a wave of American strikes on Iranian soil.

  • Brent rises more than 2% following tit-for-tat strikes by the US and Iran
    CNBCToday2 Sep

    Brent finished Tuesday 4.5% higher at $94.52 and WTI about 5% up at $90.03, the highest since 24 July, and both were adding again this morning. Crude has now retraced the entire fall it made last week on the theory that sanctions had replaced strikes.

  • US military carries out new strikes on Iranian targets
    CNN1 Sep

    Air defence sites, communications nodes and radar — the target set you choose when you intend to keep flying over a country. Trump described the strikes on Truth Social as retaliation for the attempt to lay mines in the strait and for the missile attack on the base in Jordan.

  • US strikes Iran after new Hormuz shipping attacks, Central Command says
    CNBC1 Sep

    The trigger, in Central Command’s own account: two oil tankers attacked in the strait, plus attempted attacks on American personnel. One vessel was hit by three unidentified projectiles in the southern lane near the Omani coast, without casualties — the lane that Iran and Oman have spent a fortnight negotiating over.

  • US launches new strikes against Iran as the war escalates
    Al Jazeera1 Sep

    Tehran’s side of Tuesday, including its claim of a retaliatory operation against American assets in the region and the Revolutionary Guards’ report that they downed a US MQ-9 drone over the eastern strait on Monday. Each side is now describing its own moves as answers to the other’s.

  • US says it struck IRGC targets in Iran after attacks on shipping in the Strait of Hormuz
    Euronews1 Sep

    The European framing, and the question it raises for anyone shipping through the Gulf: a corridor that Iran and Oman were jointly clearing of mines a week ago is now being mined again, according to Washington, and struck. The commercial consequence is the same whichever account is true.
