Story of the day

The ten-year Treasury yield closed at 5.041%, through 5% and at its highest since 2007 — the day before a Federal Reserve that is already more than 92% priced to raise rates again. This is not a market anticipating tightening; tightening is the assumption. It is a market demanding a larger premium to hold long American debt at all, for four reasons at once: energy prices set by a war, a Fed that has to keep moving, borrowing that nobody is restraining, and a wave of corporate issuance funding data centres. Brent settled near $108.75 with the Saudi bypass pipeline still down. Equities gave up another 0.45% on the S&P.

Finance 4

A number that has not been seen since before the financial crisis, reached the day before the Fed speaks.

  • US ten-year Treasury yield breaches 5% as the global bond sell-off deepens
    Euronews15 Sep

    The word doing the work is “global”. This is not an American repricing that happens to show up elsewhere: long yields are rising across major markets together, driven by energy costs, expected central-bank tightening, war uncertainty and government borrowing that no one is proposing to cut.

  • Treasury yields are above 5%. Here’s what it means for stocks
    CNBC15 Sep

    Mechanically, a risk-free 5% raises the bar every equity has to clear, and it does most damage to the companies whose value sits furthest in the future. Tuesday fitted that exactly: the Nasdaq fell 0.78% to 25,981.57 against the Dow’s 0.63%, with the S&P 500 at 7,585.73.

  • The beaten-up bond market may be nearing ‘escape velocity’
    CNBC15 Sep

    The contrary case, and worth having beside the panic: at 5% the coupon is large enough that an investor can absorb further rises and still come out ahead over a holding period. Whether that is courage or catching a falling knife depends entirely on where inflation settles, which is the thing nobody can price.

  • Most markets drop as oil extends gains ahead of an expected US rate hike
    France 2415 Sep

    The global session around the same two facts. Brent for November rose almost 3% to settle at $108.75 and WTI more than 4% to $105.83, with the Saudi bypass line still out of service — so the commodity that is driving the inflation is rising into the meeting called to deal with the inflation.

Economy 2

What 5% costs a government, and how long Washington says the pipeline will be shut.

  • As the ten-year yield hits 5%, debt hawks eye a national debt spiral
    Fortune15 Sep

    The arithmetic that makes this more than a market story: every maturing bond is refinanced at the new rate, so a yield that stays here turns interest into one of the largest lines in the federal budget within a few years. The household version is already visible — the average 30-year fixed mortgage was 6.76% last week against 6.15% at the start of the year.

  • Saudi pipeline closure is a brief interruption that will last days, the US energy secretary says
    CNBC15 Sep

    Chris Wright’s estimate, given to CNBC, is the most consequential forecast of the day and also the least verifiable: the East-West line had been carrying around five million barrels a day that Hormuz could not. The oil market is not taking the reassurance at face value, which is what a $108 Brent price in the same session means.

Two headlines. No statistical agency released anything on 15 September, and the Federal Reserve decision — the week’s actual economic event — comes this evening, so it belongs in tomorrow’s edition rather than being anticipated in this one.

AI 2

The safety conversation turns out to have been going on privately for weeks, and the competitive threat to the labs is arriving from an unexpected direction.

  • OpenAI, Anthropic and Google have been in talks on AI safety for weeks
    TechCrunch15 Sep

    OpenAI’s global policy chief Chris Lehane said the three have been coordinating for weeks, and that OpenAI backs a provision of the FRONTIER Act requiring frontier labs to admit “independent verification organizations”. It reframes the weekend’s essays: what looked like three rivals independently arriving at the same conclusion was a negotiated position being rolled out.

  • Salesforce and Nvidia’s new reasoning model is everything the AI labs should fear
    TechCrunch15 Sep

    Koa is Salesforce’s first reasoning model, built on Nvidia’s open-weight Nemotron and post-trained jointly for sales, marketing and support work. The threat is not the benchmark score: it is that a company with the customer relationships can now take an open base model and make something good enough, without paying anyone’s API bill.

Both from one outlet, which is the per-section limit rather than a preference; The Register, Axios, Fortune, CNBC and Euronews filed nothing else dated 15 or 16 September that qualified. Iran War 2

Saudi Arabia is now being hit from two directions and selling less oil than it has contracted to.

  • US oil tops $105 as Saudi Arabia reportedly cancels some crude cargoes after the pipeline closure
    CNBC15 Sep

    Cancelled cargoes are the hard evidence that the shutdown is biting: the kingdom is declining sales it would otherwise make. Alongside it, Houthi drones and ballistic missiles struck Khamis Mushait, Abha and Taif — so the country whose pipeline was hit from Iraq is simultaneously under fire from Yemen.

  • US says the Saudi pipeline will resume as fighting rages in Yemen
    Al Jazeera15 SepLive blog

    CENTCOM publicly rejected an Iranian claim that a supertanker had struck mines in Hormuz. Note what that dispute is about: not whether a ship was damaged but whether the strait is mined, which determines whether any insurer will write a policy for it. The contested fact is the one the whole reopening depends on.
