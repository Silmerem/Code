Story of the day

Iran retaliated across four countries. After a night of American strikes on southern Iran, Tehran fired at US bases in Jordan, Bahrain, Kuwait and Erbil in Iraq — Jordan says it intercepted ten missiles, Kuwait and Bahrain used air defences against drones. Iran’s health minister put Wednesday’s toll at 18 dead and 108 wounded, and it has risen since. Trump had warned Iran would be “totally wiped out” if it answered; it answered anyway, and he now says the US can hit Iran “anytime we want”. Deterrence that has been tested and failed is no longer deterrence.

Finance 2

Stocks went up on the day the war spread to four more countries, because the bond market paused.

  • Stocks rise as Treasury yields take a breather from their run to multiyear highs
    CNBC2 SepLive updates

    The S&P added 0.46% to 7,666.60, the Dow 295 points, the Nasdaq 0.45%. Underneath, the 10-year touched 4.818% intraday — a level unseen since November 2023 — before easing back. Equities rallied not because the news improved but because one number stopped rising.

  • US-Iran strikes send oil and yields surging as the Fed turns hawkish
    CNBC2 Sep

    WTI settled around $91.01 and Brent $95.63, both up about 1% after Tuesday’s much larger jump. Brent is now roughly 30% above where it started the war. The uncomfortable arithmetic for the Fed: every barrel of that is inflation it has said it will raise rates to fight.

Economy 2

Hiring is stalling and the Fed is arguing about whether the bond market is a warning or a compliment.

  • August hiring was tepid as the jobs report looms
    Axios2 Sep

    ADP counted 38,000 private jobs added in August, below the roughly 47,000 expected and the smallest gain since January. The composition matters more than the total: education and health services added 45,000 and leisure 16,000, while manufacturing shed 17,000 and professional services 16,000. Growth is concentrating in the sectors least exposed to interest rates.

  • New York Fed's Williams says the yield surge is down to strong economic prospects
    CNBC2 Sep

    Not dysfunction but optimism, he argued, “fueled by big investments in AI and data centers and technology in general” — and he is taking a wait-and-see view on a rise. That is a notably calmer reading than the deficit-and-war explanation this brief has been reporting for a fortnight. Markets put a September rise at about 66%.

Iran War 3

Four countries hit in one night, and both sides promising more.

  • Trump threatens more strikes as the death toll in Iran rises
    Al JazeeraToday3 Sep

    Iran’s health minister said on Wednesday that 18 had been killed and 108 wounded across several provinces; the count has risen since and this page is being updated. Trump says the US can strike “anytime we want” with a larger attack “waiting in the wings”; the supreme leader says the armed forces have “unforgettable lessons” in store.

  • US completes fresh strikes on Iran as Middle East tensions flare again
    CNBC2 Sep

    The retaliation reached further than any so far: American bases in Jordan, Bahrain, Kuwait and Erbil in northern Iraq. Jordan reported intercepting ten missiles; Kuwait and Bahrain scrambled air defences against drones. Four host governments have now had a war fought over their territory that none of them chose.

  • Iran fires on US allies in the Gulf after a night of American strikes it says killed four at a wedding
    Fortune2 Sep

    Tehran says shrapnel hit a wedding celebration in the coastal village of Kouhestak, killing four including a four-year-old and wounding at least 68. That account is Iran’s and has not been independently verified; it is reported here as a claim because it is now central to how the war is being justified on both sides.
