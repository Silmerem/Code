Story of the day

Three things happened yesterday that do not fit together. The American president said the United States would “have Iran finished very soon”. Eight foreign ministers meeting in Istanbul — including Britain’s, France’s and Germany’s — said publicly that none of them supports another attack on Iran. And Washington quietly moved diplomats and their families out of several Middle Eastern countries. The first is rhetoric, the second is diplomacy, and the third is the one that costs something to do. When a government’s words and its logistics disagree, the logistics are usually the forecast.

Economy 2

Europe goes into winter with the least gas it has ever had in store.

  • EU entering winter with lowest gas reserves on record, IEA chief warns
    Al Jazeera23 Oct

    The single most consequential line in this edition for anyone who pays a heating bill. Storage going into the cold months is the buffer that decides whether a January cold snap is an inconvenience or a price event, and this year the buffer is the thinnest on record. The 20% rise in British energy bills forecast for January was priced before this warning, not after it.

  • Are global oil reserves sufficient to withstand a renewed US-Iran war?
    Al Jazeera23 OctExplainer

    The question the oil price has been asking all week, set out properly rather than left as a market reflex. It belongs in Economy rather than in the war section below because strategic reserves are a policy instrument, not a battlefield: what they can absorb is decided by governments years in advance, and that decision is now being tested.

    Al Arabiya23 Oct

    A first, announced by Paris, between the only two nuclear powers in western Europe — and announced in the same week both put forces into the defence of Saudi Arabia. Two states that have spent sixty years keeping their deterrents separate are integrating the exercise of them, which is a larger decision than the operational one it is wrapped in. Iran War 4

Allies urging restraint, a president promising the opposite, and embassies thinning out.

  • European and Middle Eastern ministers urge US-Iran diplomacy, not new attacks
    Al Jazeera23 Oct

    Britain, France, Germany and Italy meeting Turkey, Saudi Arabia, Pakistan and Egypt in Istanbul. Turkey’s foreign minister said none of the eight approved another military intervention against Iran. Four of those eight are European allies of the United States and several of the rest host American forces; saying so in public, together, is the strongest signal this group has available short of refusing basing rights.

  • Trump says the US will ‘have Iran finished very soon’
    Al Arabiya23 Oct

    Said the same day as the Istanbul communiqué above, and reported here from a Gulf newsroom reading it as a statement of intent rather than a turn of phrase. The brief is quoting the words and not interpreting the timetable: “very soon” has no date in it, and the reporting elsewhere this week put the planning after the 3 November midterms.

  • US discreetly moves diplomats and families from multiple Middle Eastern countries as escalation fears grow
    CNN23 Oct

    Beyond Saudi Arabia, where the departure of non-emergency staff was ordered openly earlier in the week. “Discreetly” is the word doing the work: an ordered departure is a message, a quiet one is a preparation, and a government that is preparing in several countries at once is not expecting the next escalation to stay in one of them.

  • Pakistan completes ratification of the Mecca Defense Alliance agreement
    Al Arabiya23 Oct

    The pact with Saudi Arabia and Turkey is now through Pakistan’s parliament, confirmed by its foreign ministry on Friday alongside a condemnation of the Houthi attacks. A nuclear-armed state has finished the paperwork on a mutual defence commitment to a country being struck most days this month. That is the development in this section with the longest half-life.
