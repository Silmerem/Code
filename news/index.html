Story of the day

The AI story has become a credit story. CoreWeave carries about $35.6 billion of debt and a further $16.3 billion of operating lease obligations, and burned $5.7 billion of free cash flow in its latest quarter; its bonds sold off for a second day to yields above 15% and the cost of insuring them against default went to roughly 1,100 basis points. Banks are now pulling back from the sector. None of this is about whether the models work. It is the same arithmetic that runs through the rest of this edition: wholesale prices rose 6.3% on the year, so money costs more, and an industry financing twenty-year assets on borrowed money finds that out before anyone else does.

Finance 3

Lenders are the ones setting the pace of the AI build-out now, and they have started saying no.

  • Banks are pulling back as AI’s massive debt pile disconnects from economic reality
    Axios16 Oct

    The constraint Broadcom’s chief executive named on Monday arriving four days later. When the marginal lender withdraws, spending plans are revised whatever the engineering says — and most of this year’s announced capacity has been financed rather than funded from earnings.

  • CoreWeave: a look inside the $52 billion debt and lease hole the company needs to beat
    Fortune16 OctAnalysis

    $35.6 billion of debt plus $16.3 billion of lease obligations against negative free cash flow of $5.7 billion in a quarter. The company’s answer is that 98% of revenue sits in committed take-or-pay contracts, and its chief executive has told staff the market sometimes “misunderstands and acts irrationally”. Both can be true and still leave a refinancing date that does not move.

  • US Treasury yields waver as traders wrap up a volatile week
    CNBC16 Oct

    The 10-year at 5.085% and the 30-year at 5.434%, with futures putting the chance of the Fed holding this month above 80%. Worth pausing on that first number: nine days ago the 10-year touched 5.365%, its highest since 2002, and it has since fallen nearly thirty basis points in a week when inflation data came in hot twice. Money is moving into government bonds because of what it is moving out of.

Economy 2

The Fed chair had two hot inflation prints behind him and said nothing about what he will do with them.

  • Fed’s Warsh hails economic resilience but sees a ‘bumpy’ AI road ahead
    Euronews16 Oct

    Speaking in Bangkok, the chair called the global economy surprisingly resilient and expects AI to lift productivity over time, with a rough passage first. He gave no signal on the October decision. A central banker who declines to steer twelve days before a meeting is either genuinely undecided or does not want the market to price it, and this week has given him reasons for both.

  • Analysis: everyone expects the Fed to hold rates in October. Kevin Warsh could surprise us all
    CNBC16 OctAnalysis

    The case for a surprise: consumer prices at 3.7%, wholesale prices at 6.3%, one-year inflation expectations at their highest since May 2023, and a chair who has spent two years arguing that central banks move too late. Against it, a labour market that produced 29,000 jobs in September. Both halves are real, which is why the odds swung from about a fifth to about a half and back within two days.

AI 3

A sector repricing, and a peace offering to the mathematicians.

  • Why CoreWeave’s woes matter to the market
    Axios16 Oct

    Because it is the test case. CoreWeave rents out compute bought with debt, which is the structure underneath a large share of the industry’s announced capacity. Nvidia holds a $3.3 billion stake and Jensen Huang says he sees no need to step in — “I’m optimistic” — which is a statement about Nvidia’s intentions as much as about CoreWeave’s solvency.

  • CNBC Daily Open: is the AI sector losing its rose-coloured glow?
    CNBC16 Oct

    Twenty of the largest AI and semiconductor stocks have shed more than a trillion dollars of value this week. The week began with Anthropic reporting 46% quarterly revenue growth and adjusted profitability. Both of those happened, and the market has decided the second one does not answer the first.

  • After the AI maths backlash, OpenAI pledges $5m to a mathematics foundation
    Euronews16 Oct

    Part of a $15 million package with the Simons Foundation and Anthropic, after the Association for Human Mathematics objected to how machine-generated proofs are being credited. The live dispute is sharper than the funding: two mathematicians say they were close to a similar result and question whether OpenAI’s work drew on theirs, and OpenAI says it never saw their work. Money does not settle that. See the disclosure below.

Iran War 2

Riyadh names Tehran directly, and ships are going dark in the strait without it helping.

  • Iran is backing and encouraging escalated Houthi attacks: senior Saudi diplomat
    Al Arabiya16 Oct

    Saudi Arabia putting the charge on the record, four days after Tehran’s foreign minister offered to mediate between Riyadh and Sanaa. The diplomat also sets out the bargain under discussion: safe passage for commercial shipping through Hormuz in exchange for Washington lifting its naval blockade, which he calls one of two remaining obstacles to a deal. That is the most specific public description of the negotiation this brief has seen.

  • Iran war updates: ships in the Strait of Hormuz still face ‘indiscriminate attacks’, a maritime monitor warns
    CBS News16 OctLive blog

    Four vessels struck on Tuesday and six on Sunday, and the monitor’s finding is that switching off tracking transponders is no longer protecting ships. A crew hiding its position and being hit anyway means the targeting is not discriminating between vessels, which is the condition under which insurers stop writing cover and a waterway closes commercially without anyone announcing it.

Two headlines. Al Jazeera, CNN, NPR and France 24 filed nothing on the war dated 16 or 17 October; the coalition says Riyadh’s airport has been attacked twenty times this month and it was due to stay shut until today, with no reopening announcement this brief could find. The CBS page carries no date in its address and is placed from the strike counts in its own text.
