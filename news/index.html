Story of the day

Yesterday the oil market priced peace: Brent fell 7.63% to $104.77, having settled above $117 four days earlier, on a third day of Iranian-Qatari talks. This morning the United States is reported to have struck Iran’s Qeshm Island, and the same report relays a Wall Street Journal account that Washington has been running limited strikes without announcing them. The president promised no attacks before the midterms. Americans vote today. Both of those statements can be true at once only if an unannounced strike does not count as an attack, which is a definition the market was not given when it sold oil off 7%.

Finance 2

Oil gave back a month in a day, and Apple had a very good quarter.

  • Crude prices slide as investors brace for a big week
    CNBC2 Nov

    Brent down 7.63% to $104.77 and WTI down 7.36% to $96.46, on diplomacy rather than on any change in supply. Four days earlier the same benchmark settled at a six-month high above $117. A commodity that moves 7% on the tone of a negotiation is not telling you about barrels; it is telling you how much of its price is politics.

  • Apple earnings: iPhone sales lead revenue upward
    Axios2 Nov

    Revenue of $114.5 billion, up 11.7%, net income of $30.7 billion, 57.6 million iPhones, and Greater China up more than 20% to $17.5 billion — reported under a new chief executive, with the company having retaken the title of most valuable in the world from Nvidia. In a fortnight that punished every company explaining how much it intends to spend on AI, the one that said least about it was rewarded most.

Economy 1

Bond yields did not follow oil down, which is the interesting part.

  • Treasury yields are mixed as oil prices tumble ahead of the midterms
    CNBC2 Nov

    The 10-year off a couple of basis points to 4.906%, the 30-year down three to 5.279%, the two-year slightly up at 4.594%. For most of this year oil and yields moved together, because the war was the inflation story; that link came apart in October when the market found a second thing to worry about. A 7% fall in crude that moves the long bond by three basis points is the clearest evidence yet that it has.

Iran War 4

Strikes this morning, a two-day plan for Hormuz, and an election today.

  • US strikes Iran’s Qeshm Island as Trump signals progress in talks
    France 243 NovToday

    A strike on an island at the mouth of the Strait of Hormuz on the morning of the midterms, alongside a presidential comment that a deal “could happen”, and a Wall Street Journal report relayed here that a series of American strikes has been carried out without public announcement. The pledge was no attacks before the elections. Hold the two together and the pledge was about announcements.

  • Iran says it is considering a two-day plan to reopen Hormuz
    Al Arabiya2 Nov

    Down from a week, on conditions that have not changed: lift the naval blockade of Iranian ports and repeal sanctions. Tehran shortening its own timetable while holding its terms is a concession on theatre and not on substance — but it is the concession that moved the oil price 7%.

  • Trump signals progress on an Iran deal as Tehran pushes a faster ceasefire
    Al Jazeera2 Nov

    The same negotiation from the other side of the table, and worth both links because the emphasis differs: Washington’s progress against Tehran’s urgency. A party that wants a ceasefire sooner is usually the party that expects the next month to go badly for it.

  • UK pledges action to support Saudi Arabia amid Houthi attacks
    Al Arabiya2 Nov

    Britain’s Middle East minister calling the attacks on Saudi Arabia and inside Yemen unacceptable and demanding they stop. The RAF has been flying defensive missions over the kingdom since late October; a minister promising “action” after that has already spent the easy version of the word.
