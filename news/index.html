Story of the day

One day after the AI industry signed a voluntary accord to police itself, a federal regulator opened an investigation into it. The Federal Trade Commission is examining OpenAI, Anthropic and other AI companies over the risks their products pose. The accord Trump announced on Tuesday — the White House Accord on Super Intelligence, signed by OpenAI, Anthropic, Google, Meta, xAI and Nvidia — runs to a single page and commits each firm to audit itself under its own directors’ supervision. Dario Amodei signed it and told reporters it was only “a start”, with the mechanism “still under discussion”. The photograph of the signed pledge that the president posted to Truth Social misspells “United States”.

Economy 1

Inflation came in cooler than forecast and long yields rose anyway.

  • Ten-year Treasury yield is higher as traders look past inflation data and await the jobs report
    CNBC30 Sep

    Core PCE rose 3% on the year against a 3.3% forecast and the headline measure 3.4%, both below expectations — and the ten-year still added four basis points to 5.298%, breaching 5.3% during the session, with the thirty-year above 5.6%. When a soft inflation print cannot pull long yields down, the market is pricing something other than inflation.

One headline, and the week’s significant release is still ahead: the American jobs report, which lands into a Fed that has already moved once and a bond market that is not waiting for it.

AI 5

A self-regulation pact on Tuesday, a federal investigation on Wednesday, and a typo in the middle of it.

  • FTC is investigating OpenAI, Anthropic and other AI companies over product risks
    CNBC30 Sep

    The timing is the story. Twenty-four hours after the president hosted the same companies and announced that self-policing was the American answer, a federal agency with subpoena powers opened an inquiry into whether their products are safe. Both can be the administration’s position; only one of them compels anybody to produce documents.

  • Trump’s AI ‘constitution’ crowns a day of accelerating ambition
    Axios30 Sep

    The text itself: one page, signed by OpenAI, Anthropic, Google, Meta, xAI and Nvidia, promising “four layers of controls and auditing” with outside evaluators and independent board oversight, and stating that “every company is responsible for developing its own technology safely”. Amodei called it “a start”; Zuckerberg called it confidence-building. Those are not the same claim.

  • Unlike the EU, Trump’s new AI pact lets tech companies police themselves
    Euronews30 Sep

    The comparison that gives the accord its scale. Europe has binding obligations with penalties attached; Washington now has a voluntary undertaking with an internal committee. Companies operating in both will comply with the stricter one, which means the EU is setting the floor for American firms whatever this document says.

  • Pledge signed by President Trump and top AI leaders misspells the United States
    TechCrunch30 Sep

    “Unites States”, in the photograph the president shared of the signed page. A typo is not an argument about AI policy. But a one-page document signed by six of the most valuable companies on earth, in a ceremony staged as the alternative to legislation, went out with the country’s name wrong on it.

  • OpenAI apes AWS Marketplace while Anthropic remains stubbornly Microsoftian
    The Register30 SepOpinion

    A columnist’s read of the two firms’ distribution strategies, flagged as opinion, and a useful reminder on a week of constitutional language that these are companies with distribution strategies. One is building a marketplace; the other is selling through an incumbent’s sales force.

Iran War 2

A US proposal reached Tehran through Doha, and the argument has narrowed to sequencing.

  • Trump denies offering Iran sanctions relief; Tehran receives a US proposal after Qatar talks
    CNBC30 Sep

    Araghchi put an American proposal to the Iranian cabinet on Wednesday after meeting Qatari mediators in Doha. Reuters reports an official briefed on the talks saying the remaining disagreement is about the order of steps rather than their content — which, if right, is the most substantive movement of the war. Trump separately says it will end “very soon” and gives no detail.

  • Economic war: is Iran losing its leverage over the Strait of Hormuz?
    Al Jazeera30 Sep

    The question underneath the sequencing argument. If crude is already moving at close to pre-war volumes under naval escort, then the thing Tehran is offering to reopen is worth less than it was in February — and both negotiating teams can read the same shipping data.
