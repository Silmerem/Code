Finance 3

A quiet Monday on the tape and a loud one underneath it: the Treasury is now the biggest actor in its own bond market.

  • Washington escalates on two fronts, with Nvidia earnings in focus
    CNBCToday25 Aug

    This morning’s framing, and the right one: sanctions on Iran and fresh tariffs on Canada announced inside a day, with the market’s single most important earnings report landing tomorrow night. Monday itself was mild — the S&P off 0.28% to 7,652.86, the Dow up 0.26%, the Nasdaq down 0.76% on a chip sell-off that took Micron 5.8% lower.

  • Treasury yields rise despite government bond buybacks as markets await Jackson Hole
    Fortune24 Aug

    The week’s recurring result: an intervention is announced, yields dip, yields come back. The 30-year finished Monday around 5.25%. Everything now waits on Warsh at Jackson Hole, who is expected to try to talk the long end down with words rather than purchases.

  • How the Treasury could step up its bond market intervention
    Axios24 Aug

    The clearest short account of what is actually available to Bessent, and of the scepticism meeting it. Markets rallied on Monday’s report and then thought better of it — the doubt is not whether the money exists but whether spending it on long bonds addresses a problem that is fundamentally about supply.

Economy 2

A trillion-dollar cash pile, and one man now running both the sanctions campaign and the bond market.

  • Bessent could tap the near-$1 trillion Treasury General Account to fund bond buybacks
    CNBC24 Aug

    Two senior Treasury officials, and the number is the story: the TGA has been built up to about $950bn, against a stated target of $550–600bn under the previous administration. That build-up now looks less like prudence than ammunition. The 30-year fell four basis points to 5.23% on the report, then gave it back.

  • Bessent becomes the tip of the spear in the war on Iran and on the bond vigilantes
    Fortune24 Aug

    The piece that joins this brief’s two running stories into one. The same official is trying to raise the price of Iranian oil for everyone who buys it and lower the price the United States pays to borrow — and the first policy works against the second, because oil is what is keeping inflation expectations, and therefore yields, where they are.

AI 1

One line, but a real one — and everything else in this section is waiting for tomorrow night.

  • Nvidia says Groq racks will be online this year following the $20 billion purchase
    CNBC24 Aug

    Groq built inference chips that competed with Nvidia’s on speed and cost; Nvidia bought the company and is now putting its racks into service inside its own estate. Consistent with everything this brief has carried this month — the moat is being widened with capital rather than silicon.

Nvidia reports after the close on Wednesday, with consensus at about $92bn of quarterly revenue — roughly double a year ago. Two questions carry the whole AI trade: what Jensen Huang says about the Vera Rubin systems now shipping as samples, and whether H200 sales into China will be permitted. Two searches found nothing else filed on Monday; the section runs at one rather than reaching back.

UK Politics 2

A prime minister in Kyiv, asked about tax at home and answered from Moscow the same day.

  • Burnham refuses to rule out UK tax rises during his first official visit to Ukraine
    ITV News24 Aug

    The question that follows every prime minister abroad, and the non-answer that follows every autumn budget. It lands differently this year: gilt yields have risen with everyone else’s, and a government that has already cut VAT on energy and capped bus fares has to fund both from somewhere.

  • Kremlin adviser says UK factories could face attack from 'unknown sources'
    ITV News24 Aug

    The response to Monday’s missile announcement, and an escalation in the form of the threat rather than its content: “unknown sources” is deniability written into the warning. A week ago Moscow threatened “consequences” over British drones; now it is naming British factories.

ITV News also covered the Kyiv visit itself, which this brief carried yesterday from Sunday’s preview. The two-per-outlet cap means one of the three had to go, and repeating a story already run is the wrong one to keep.

Iran War 4

The campaign is launched, named and aimed at third countries. The question it raises is whether they comply.

  • Trump administration announces 'economic D-Day' sanctions on Iran
    Al Jazeera24 Aug

    “Operation Economic Outcast”, targeting every source of Iranian revenue including oil, and every foreign company and country that touches it. Tehran’s finance minister said Iran is “fully prepared”; Ghalibaf brushed the threats off. Iran has lived under sanctions for most of two decades, so the novelty is the reach, not the pressure.

  • US unveils expanded sanctions aimed at Iran's 'economic asphyxiation'
    France 2424 Aug

    The European read, and it dwells on the part Washington understates: secondary sanctions work by making other countries’ banks choose, which means the cost of this campaign falls first on allies and neutrals rather than on Iran. That is also why it has failed to gather partners so far.

  • Bessent signals China is not exempt from the Iran sanctions plan
    CNBC24 AugVideo

    The single most consequential sentence of the press conference. China takes about 80% of Iran’s exported oil and said on Friday that “sanctions and pressure tactics are not the solution”. Saying Beijing is not exempt commits Washington either to sanctioning Chinese banks or to being seen not to.

  • What to know about an 'economic D-Day' for Iran
    CNN24 AugVideo

    A short explainer of the mechanics, worth it if the phrase has been hard to pin down all week. Note what Bessent conceded in the same breath: Monday was a “warning shot”, and the institution-level measures come later this week. The announcement was largely an announcement.
