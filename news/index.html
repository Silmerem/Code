Story of the day

Iran’s conditions for ending the war are now public in outline, and the early American reading of them is not encouraging. Mohsen Rezaei set them out to Al Jazeera; a former Pentagon director of Arabian Peninsula affairs told the same outlet that Washington would be unlikely to treat them as a serious opening — “at first glance, probably not serious; they would be viewed as surrender”. Rezaei also said Tehran has not decided to leave the Non-Proliferation Treaty, while warning that American conduct “may push” it to. Meanwhile the oil market, which spent a fortnight pricing catastrophe, has decided the crude is getting out anyway: Brent fell below $103 this morning and American crude under $100.

  • Bessent calls the meeting with China’s He Lifeng ‘successful’ ahead of the summit

Iran War 1

The terms are out, and the first American assessment of them is “surrender”.

  • What are Iran’s new conditions to end its war with the US?
    Al Jazeera20 Sep

    Rezaei’s account, given to Al Jazeera, of terms conveyed to Washington through Qatar. On the nuclear question he said Iran has not decided to leave the Non-Proliferation Treaty despite believing it has grounds to, still honours Khamenei’s fatwa against nuclear weapons, and could be pushed out by American conduct. The counter-assessment in the same piece, from a former Pentagon official, is that the terms read as a demand for surrender rather than an opening.
