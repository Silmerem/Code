Story of the day

American inflation rose to 3.7% in September, the fastest pace since January and the last reading voters get before the midterms. Petrol was up 9.1% in a single month and accounts for about half the increase; groceries actually fell. The part this brief got wrong yesterday is the rest of it: core inflation, which strips out food and energy, came in at 2.6% rather than the 2.5% expected, and rose 0.3% on the month rather than 0.2%. So this is not purely a war premium passing through a pump. Underlying prices are moving too, and the Fed meets in thirteen days with a labour market that added 29,000 jobs and a headline it cannot reach with interest rates.

Finance 2

Bonds sold off on the core number, and the AI trade is being measured against its own arithmetic.

  • 10-year Treasury yield jumps as September core inflation comes in hotter than expected
    CNBC14 Oct

    The long end had spent two sessions coming down on stabilising energy prices; the core print reversed it inside an hour. That is the market making the same distinction the lede does — a headline driven by crude is a shock to live through, and a core rate that will not settle is a reason to keep raising.

  • The AI capex and revenue numbers that show we are approaching the top of the AI boom

    The case assembled from the companies’ own disclosures rather than from sentiment: spending commitments running far ahead of revenue, with the gap financed by debt and share warrants. “Approaching the top” is a claim nobody can verify until afterwards. The numbers underneath it are published and checkable, which is the part worth your time.

Economy 4

One number, four readings, and a cost-of-living rise for every American pensioner that follows automatically from it.

  • Inflation rose to 3.7% last month in the last snapshot of consumer prices before the midterms
    CNN14 Oct

    Prices up 0.6% on the month and 3.7% on the year, from 3.4% in August and above the 3.6% forecast, with core at 2.6% against 2.5% expected. Both halves came in hot. The political framing in the headline is not editorialising: there is no further reading before 3 November, so this is the figure the campaign now argues about.

  • Here’s the inflation breakdown for September 2026 — in one chart
    CNBC14 Oct

    Where it came from: petrol up 9.1% in the month, roughly half the entire headline rise, against groceries falling 0.1%. A household that drives is in a different economy from one that does not, and an average that nets the two together is describing neither.

  • Inflation heats up as gas prices surge
    Axios14 Oct

    The political economy of a fuel-led print three weeks before a vote. The administration has spent the month trying to pull petrol down — the G7 reserve release, the Russian diesel arrangement, pressure on Kyiv to stop hitting refineries — and September’s data is from before any of it. The October figure, published after the election, is the one that will show whether any of it worked.

  • Inflation sizzled in September — and Social Security benefits will get a boost
    NPR14 Oct

    The mechanical consequence: a 3.6% cost-of-living adjustment for 2027, announced the same day. Indexation is the one part of the American system that responds to inflation without anybody deciding anything, which makes it both the most reliable protection on offer and a straight addition to the federal deficit the CBO warned about on Saturday.

AI 3

The company that writes this brief has opened its books, and the industry’s sums still do not add up.

  • Anthropic’s third-quarter revenue is said to hit $16.8 billion ahead of an IPO
    CNBC15 OctToday

    Up from about $11.6 billion in the second quarter, with the company said to be profitable on an adjusted basis. Three things to hold together: the figures were shown to prospective investors rather than filed, “adjusted” is doing unspecified work, and it was Anthropic’s own revenue convention that made OpenAI’s number look $20 billion larger a week ago. See the disclosure below — this is a story about the maker of the software writing it.

  • The AI math isn’t mathing as infrastructure spending is forecast to outstrip expected revenue
    The Register14 Oct

    Gartner’s figures put total AI data-centre revenue at no more than $3.6 trillion against roughly $8.5 trillion of investment needed for backers to earn 12%. Forecasts over that horizon are guesses with decimal points, and the direction of the gap is the point: at these spending levels the industry needs a revenue line that nobody has yet described.

  • Taser maker Axon is mandating AI use to speed up work as its surveillance tools face scrutiny
    Fortune14 Oct

    A company that sells police surveillance requiring its own staff to use AI, while the tools it sells are under examination. The interesting part is the mandate rather than the product: compelled adoption inside a firm is how these systems reach the work that nobody chose to automate, and it leaves no record of who objected.

Iran War 4

Aramco’s headquarters city was hit, and the two accounts of the damage do not match.

  • Saudi Arabia says a ‘limited fire’ hit an Aramco building targeted in Dhahran, with no casualties
    Al Arabiya14 Oct

    The official account: an administrative building in the company’s headquarters city, a limited fire, no injuries or deaths, and no confirmation of whether it was struck directly or hit by debris from an interception. Reuters, through three people with knowledge, describes a fire spreading across several floors with staff moved to working from home. Both versions are in circulation and this brief cannot resolve them.

  • Houthis threaten escalation after a strike on Saudi oil giant Aramco
    France 2414 Oct

    The group says it fired ballistic and cruise missiles and drones at Aramco sites in Dhahran and Riyadh and at Riyadh’s airport, and that operations “will continue in the coming days, targeting Saudi enemy airports and oil facilities”. Riyadh’s airport, attacked four times now, is shut until at least 17 October. A stated programme of attacks on named civilian infrastructure is a different thing from a reprisal.

  • Saudi-led coalition intercepts a Houthi missile launched towards Riyadh
    Al Jazeera14 Oct

    The interception worked and people were still hurt: debris from two intercepted missiles wounded four in the capital and damaged fifteen homes, with fragments also falling in a residential area of Dhahran. This is the part that does not appear in air-defence statistics. A successful intercept over a city still puts metal on roofs.

  • Trump blasts ‘not nice’ Houthis as an attack hits a Saudi Aramco facility
    Euronews14 Oct

    Still weighing whether to join, five days after an American was killed at Riyadh airport, and the language has gone from “misbehaving very badly” to “not nice”. Washington is already supplying intelligence, target identification and planning for the Saudi offensive, so the decision being deliberated in public is about American ordnance rather than American involvement.

The death toll from the 10 October airport attack is still given variously as twelve, thirteen and fourteen. This brief asserted a single figure twice last week and was wrong both times; it is not asserting one now. An American citizen was killed and more than three hundred people were injured.
