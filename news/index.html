Story of the day

Washington spent Friday trying to recruit the rest of the world into its economic war, and the one country that matters said no. China buys about 80% of Iran’s exported oil; asked to join the campaign, Beijing’s foreign ministry replied that “sanctions and pressure tactics are not the solution”. Tehran’s foreign minister called the “Economic D-Day” a distraction from “America’s own crisis: unprecedented debt & surging interest costs” — a jibe that lands harder than usual in a week when the Treasury had to intervene in its own bond market and the intervention failed inside a day.

Finance 2

Two assets that rise when people stop trusting government paper both had a very good week.

  • Bitcoin surges 22% for the week as investor optimism floods back
    CNBC21 Aug

    It closed Friday up 6% at $76,943.90, against about $62,800 on Monday. Robinhood rose almost 14% on the day, Coinbase 8%, Strategy 29% on the week. Two things did it: the Clarity Act push, and roughly $2.7bn of short positions liquidated — which means a good part of a 22% week is mechanical, not conviction.

  • Current price of gold: August 21, 2026
    Fortune21 Aug

    $4,587 an ounce, up $115 in a single day and $3,344 over the year. A $115 daily move in gold is not a hedge being adjusted, it is a bid. Set it beside a 30-year Treasury stuck above 5% and the message is the same one twice: the safest asset is no longer the one governments issue.

Economy 2

The buyback rally is now completely gone, and the household cost of that is visible in the mortgage market.

  • Longer-dated Treasury yields rise as Bessent's bond buyback rally fizzles out
    CNBC21 Aug

    The 30-year finished the week at 5.2371% and the 10-year at 4.6882%, both flat on the day and both above where they stood before Wednesday’s announcement. Three sessions is the whole life of that intervention. The operations themselves do not begin until 9 September, so what is being priced now is the market’s advance verdict on them.

  • Current refinance mortgage rates report for Aug. 21, 2026
    Fortune21 Aug

    A 30-year fixed refinance is averaging 6.750%. The piece traces the path plainly: rates jumped in March with Operation Epic Fury, dipped briefly on June’s ceasefire, and ticked back up when it collapsed in July. Whatever the bond market thinks of the Treasury, this is where households actually meet the war.

Iran War 3

An economic campaign only works if other countries join it. On Friday, the biggest one declined.

  • Key Iranian trading partner China rejects Trump's 'economic D-Day' threat
    CNN21 AugLive blog

    Beijing takes about 80% of Iran’s shipped oil, and its foreign ministry answered that “sanctions and pressure tactics are not the solution”. This is not new ground: when the US sanctioned five Chinese “teapot” refineries earlier this year, China issued an injunction telling them to ignore it.

  • US warns allies and China to join the Iran economic pressure campaign
    France 2421 Aug

    The threat is secondary sanctions on any country whose banks, businesses or airports give Iran a “lifeline”. What is being attempted unilaterally here normally requires the Security Council; doing it by ultimatum means the campaign’s success now depends on states that have already said no.

  • Iran and Oman discuss Hormuz as Tehran dismisses Trump's threats
    Al Jazeera21 AugLive blog

    Foreign minister Araghchi called the sanctions a diversion from “America’s own crisis: unprecedented debt & surging interest costs”; state media called the announcement “not a new development”. The substantive line is buried under the rhetoric: Iran and Oman are still talking about the strait, which is the only channel currently open.

The strait itself has not moved. Iran’s parliamentary speaker has said it stays shut until the June memorandum is honoured — blockade lifted, oil sanctions removed, assets unfrozen — and tanker transits remain near three-month lows.
