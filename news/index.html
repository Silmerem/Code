Story of the day

Two ballistic missiles were fired at Saudi Arabia yesterday and there are three accounts of where they were aimed. A Saudi outlet reports them intercepted on their way to Mecca and Medina, with debris falling in Mecca itself. The Saudi-led coalition’s own statement names Yanbu and Taif. The Houthis call the holy-cities version a “huge lie” and say they hit three airports, oil infrastructure and an airbase. This brief cannot tell you which is true. What is not in dispute is that Saudi civil defence put out alerts for Mecca and Jeddah, and that a group which spent this war promising to spare the two holy cities is now being accused of aiming at them — an accusation that, true or false, is worth more to Riyadh than any airport.

Finance 1

The credit market has started pricing AI companies the way it prices distress.

  • Here’s the ‘core economic flaw’ in the AI bull case as stock and bond trades unravel, according to a top economist
    Fortune24 OctAnalysis

    Apollo’s chief economist argues that AI has delivered no measurable financial payoff to American companies outside the handful building the infrastructure. The numbers alongside it are the sharper point: CoreWeave down more than 30% this month with its five-year credit default swaps at 1,145 basis points, and Meta’s at a record 114.79 after widening 7.6 in a day. Equity markets argue about valuations. Credit markets price the odds of not being repaid, and this week they moved.

AI 2

The chip maker is now also the lender, and Washington has opened a safety investigation into the two largest labs.

  • Nvidia’s role as AI financier faces its first big test as neoclouds take a market hit
    CNBC24 Oct

    CoreWeave, Nebius and IREN — all cash-burning companies Nvidia holds stakes in — are down at least 30% this month, and Nvidia itself is off 7.6% in October. It has also guaranteed up to $105 billion for a proposed OpenAI data centre in Ohio, with the right to take over the lease, find another tenant or force a sale if the payments stop. A supplier that finances its own customers has turned its revenue into a loan book, and a loan book is tested in exactly this kind of month.

  • Washington’s message to AI executives: please shut up
    Axios24 Oct

    The day after a White House lunch at which the industry promised to police itself, the Federal Trade Commission disclosed a broad safety investigation into OpenAI and Anthropic, and legislators criticised Anthropic over safety incidents it had not disclosed. Read the colophon below before you weigh my summary of this one: Anthropic makes the software that wrote it, and this is the second consecutive edition in which the news about that company is adverse.

UK Politics 1

Three days to the Budget, and the chancellor is offering business something that costs nothing.

  • Healey plans to slash ‘pointless paperwork’ in a bid to save businesses £800m
    ITV News22 Oct

    A deregulation package announced before a Budget at which the same chancellor is expected to raise the surcharge on bank profits. £800 million of saved administration against a fiscal gap the IFS puts at £35 billion is not a substitute and is not offered as one; it is the part of a pro-business message that does not have to be paid for. Carried from Thursday under this section’s wider window because no British newsroom this brief can reach filed a political story over the weekend.

Iran War 4

A heavy Saturday of strikes, a dispute about the holy cities, and Tehran drawing a line at sea.

  • Houthis launch attacks on Saudi Arabia and Yemen’s Aden
    Al Jazeera24 Oct

    Strikes on both the kingdom and the seat of Yemen’s internationally recognised government on the same day. In Aden the government says two missiles hit a coast guard headquarters and a third a coast guard building in Tawahi; the Houthis describe their targets as a military camp and a naval base. The second half of this war — the Yemeni one — gets a fraction of the coverage of the Saudi half and is where most of the dying is.

  • Debris from two intercepted Houthi ballistic missiles falls in Mecca
    Al Arabiya24 Oct

    The Saudi account: two missiles aimed at Mecca and Medina, intercepted, with wreckage coming down at Wadi al-Mughammas. The coalition’s own statement the same day named Yanbu and Taif as the targets, and civil defence issued alerts for Taif, Yanbu, Mecca and Jeddah. Those two versions cannot both be complete, and this brief is carrying the discrepancy rather than resolving it.

  • Yemen’s Houthi rebels claim new attacks on oil facilities and airports across Saudi Arabia
    France 2424 Oct

    The third version: the Houthis say they struck three airports, oil infrastructure and an airbase, and call the holy-cities claim a “huge lie”. They have promised through this war to spare Mecca and Medina, which gives them a reason to deny it whether or not it happened — and gives Riyadh a reason to say it whether or not it happened. Three accounts, no independent verification, and the oil price will trade on whichever one sticks.

  • Iran says it won’t allow US warships to get close as Trump ponders new attacks
    Al Jazeera24 Oct

    Tehran’s answer to a week in which the American president said the United States would “have Iran finished very soon” and Washington quietly thinned its embassies across the Gulf. A declared exclusion zone that neither side defines precisely is the kind of commitment that gets tested by accident before it gets tested on purpose.
