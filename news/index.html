Story of the day

“At dawn begins an economic D-Day.” That was the Treasury secretary on X on Sunday evening, trailing a sanctions package he calls the endgame and “the single greatest financial offensive ever” imposed on any country; the press conference is today. Tehran spent Sunday preparing its answer at sea — parliament approved charging ships for passage through Hormuz, and the state strait authority warned that vessels breaking its transit rules face “fines, seizure, or confiscation”. One side is turning the banking system into a weapon, the other a waterway.

  • Mayors to get new powers to overrule local councils on major planning projects
    ITV News23 Aug

Iran War 4

Sanctions today, ship seizures threatened in return, and a president in Tehran saying out loud that this cannot go on.

  • Live: the US is set to unveil its 'economic D-Day' sanctions on Iran
    CNNToday24 AugLive blog

    Bessent’s press conference is today, billed as “the greatest campaign of coordinated economic isolation in the history of the world”. The measures add to a regime already covering Iranian banking, energy, aviation and crypto — which is the awkward part of calling it an endgame: most of what can be sanctioned already has been, and the new weight falls on third countries.

  • Iran warns of Hormuz ship seizures ahead of Bessent's sanctions push
    CNBCToday24 Aug

    The state-run Persian Gulf Strait Authority says vessels breaking its transit rules face “fines, seizure, or confiscation” on future passages. Read alongside Saturday’s permission for Iraqi tankers, this is a coherent policy rather than a bluff: Tehran is building an administration for the strait — permits, fees, penalties — and daring the world to treat it as illegitimate.

  • Iranian parliament advances plans for Hormuz service fees
    Al Jazeera23 AugLive blog

    Approved Sunday: ships passing the chokepoint will pay Iran for services rendered. Also in this thread, and more important than the fees, President Pezeshkian saying Iran “cannot continue with war forever” and defending the June memorandum against the supreme leader’s reported misgivings. That is a split in Tehran being aired in public, six months into the war.

  • Can Iran retaliate against countries that join the US 'economic war'?
    Al Jazeera23 AugAnalysis

    The question Rezaei’s Saturday threat raised and did not answer. Useful because it separates what Iran can actually do — to Gulf shipping, to Gulf infrastructure, to states within missile range — from what it is merely saying, and because the answer determines whether Bessent’s campaign finds the partners it needs.
