Finance 2

Two soft inflation prints lifted the market all week. One weak consumer took the shine off on Friday.

  • Dollar falls on surprise drop in US retail sales
    CNBC14 Aug

    The currency market read the retail miss as growth risk rather than inflation relief, and sold the dollar. The yen’s slide to a weekly loss has traders positioning for another Tokyo intervention — the second this month.

  • 'It's not lights out for the economy': US retail sales drop 0.6% in July
    Fortune14 Aug

    The quote is an economist talking the number down, and the caveats are real — Prime Day fell in June, so online sales were always going to drop. But 0.6% against an expected 0.1% gain is the weakest month in over a year.

Economy 4

Prices cooled twice this week and the consumer stopped spending anyway. That combination is what a supply shock looks like from the household end.

  • Retail spending declined in July for the first time in months
    NPR14 Aug

    Down 0.6%, and broad: car dealers off 2%, petrol stations 0.9%, electronics 0.5%. Clothing, furniture and building supplies rose — the pattern of a household deferring the big purchase rather than one that has stopped buying.

  • Consumer sentiment dips, especially among Republicans
    Axios14 Aug

    Michigan’s index fell to 51.0 from 55.2, and year-ahead inflation expectations rose to 4.3% — against 3.4% before the war. Sentiment souring fastest among the president’s own voters is the political fact inside an economic release.

  • Retail sales unexpectedly fall in July as government tax refunds fade
    The Hill14 Aug

    The mechanism nobody was pricing: the tax refunds that propped up spring spending have run out. That makes July less a verdict on confidence than the moment a temporary support was withdrawn.

  • We learned 5 important things about the economy this week
    NPR14 Aug

    The week assembled: consumer prices at 3.4%, wholesale prices flat, retail sales down, sentiment down, and wages still rising more slowly than prices. Cooling inflation and a cooling consumer are the same story told twice.

UK Politics 3

Farage won his seat back and the standards investigation he interrupted picked up where it left off. His own party is the one asking questions.

  • Nigel Farage wins Clacton by-election as standards inquiry resumes
    ITV News14 Aug

    22,239 votes to Count Binface’s 9,455. And the sentence that undoes the point of the exercise: the parliamentary standards inquiry into his undeclared donations, suspended when he resigned, resumes now he is an MP again.

  • Reform UK splits burst into open despite Nigel Farage's Clacton by-election victory
    Yahoo News UK14 Aug

    Senior figures are openly baffled that their leader skipped his own declaration. Tim Montgomerie: “You don’t skip your own victory count without a much better explanation.” The criticism is coming from inside the party, not from the boycotting opposition.

  • Andy Burnham dealt another by-election blow just hours after Nigel Farage cruises to Clacton win
    GB News14 Aug

    The council contests nobody covered: the Liberal Democrats took a seat off Labour and another off Reform on the same night. Two data points against a national poll picture that has Labour recovering — small, but they are actual votes.

Iran War 2

A claim of sovereignty over an international waterway, made at a rally, on the eve of the deadline that was supposed to produce a deal.

  • Iran war live: Trump says he will declare Hormuz US territory 'pretty soon'
    Al JazeeraToday15 AugLive blog

    This morning’s thread. Legal specialists quoted throughout make the same point: transit tolls and territorial claims over a strait used for international navigation do not survive contact with the law of the sea. Which does not mean the blockade ends.

  • Trump says he'll 'never apologize' for military action against Tehran
    CNN14 AugLive blog

    Friday’s thread, and the numbers underneath the rhetoric: 151 ships in or out of the strait in seven days, and 56 reports of damage to vessels there since the war began in February. A waterway that carried about 130 ships a day is now carrying twenty.

Also on Friday, and not summarised above for want of a datable source: a Houthi strike on the Yemeni port of Mokha killed six, and Saudi Arabia condemned attacks on two ADNOC tankers in the strait, blaming Iran. The June memorandum brokered by Pakistan and Qatar expires tomorrow.
