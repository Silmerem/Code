Finance 4

Crude gave back months of war premium and the dollar’s slide against the yen was halted by force — two of the largest single-day moves of the year, both traceable to the same Iran headline.

  • US Treasury yields fall as oil prices plunge on Iran de-escalation hopes
    CNBCToday3 Aug

    Yields retreated as crude sold off on the prospect of talks restarting. The move rests entirely on a de-escalation that Tehran spent the afternoon denying.

  • Oil prices sink on Middle East hopes, yen extends gains after joint intervention
    France 24Today3 Aug

    Crude futures slid more than 6% at the Asian open, with WTI down 4.7% to $80.72. The one wire story tying together the day’s two big moves — oil and the yen.

  • Japan and US confirm rare joint intervention to prop up yen
    Al JazeeraToday3 Aug

    The first coordinated Tokyo–Washington action since 2011. The dollar fell to 157.84 yen from a near 40-year low of 164 last week, and both sides said they would step in again if needed.

  • Stocks rally, fuelled by gains in Big Tech and a drop in oil prices
    CNBC2 AugLive blog

    The Dow closed 681 points higher (1.3%), the S&P 500 up 1.6% and the Nasdaq up 2.3%. Meta rose almost 6% and Amazon touched a $3 trillion market capitalisation.

Economy 2

Two policy interventions landed on the same day: OPEC+ completed its supply rollback, and the US Treasury joined Tokyo in the currency market for the first time in fifteen years.

  • US dollar weakens sharply against the Japanese yen after market interventions
    NPRToday3 Aug

    Why Washington acted on a currency it normally leaves alone: a yen at 164 was making Japanese exports cheap and American goods expensive, at a moment when neither government wanted a trade fight on top of an energy shock.

  • Markets exhale as Trump steps back and OPEC+ steps up
    CNBCToday3 Aug

    OPEC+ approved roughly 188,000 more barrels a day from September, completing the rollback of the 1.65m bpd cut agreed in 2023. Extra supply arriving just as the war premium deflates.

AI 4

Both sides of the Atlantic moved on frontier-model oversight within a day of each other — Brussels with binding powers and fines, Washington with a meeting and a request.

  • OpenAI and Anthropic face new scrutiny as EU AI Act enforcement powers kick in
    CNBCToday3 Aug

    The Commission can now inspect models, demand evaluations before EU release, restrict market access and levy fines. Rules for high-risk uses in hiring, credit and healthcare became fully binding today.

  • White House to meet OpenAI, Anthropic and other top AI companies in first big regulation push
    CNN BusinessToday3 Aug

    Tuesday’s meeting covers a framework for reviewing frontier models before launch. Unlike Brussels, Washington is asking rather than compelling: submission would be voluntary, up to 30 days ahead of release.

  • EU rules on AI models become enforceable. What's going to change?
    Euronews2 Aug

    The practical guide to what took effect: transparency and documentation duties for advanced models, with penalties reaching 7% of global annual revenue for providers that ignore them.

  • Nvidia is about to spend $750 billion on AI. Critics are calling it a bubble
    NPR2 Aug

    The case that the build-out is financing itself in a circle: Nvidia backstops OpenAI’s data centres, AMD puts $5bn into Anthropic which then buys AMD chips, and Google and Amazon fund a customer for their own silicon.
