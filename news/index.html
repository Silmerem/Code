Story of the day

Six days after the chairman said the Fed had “work to do”, a governor said the opposite. Christopher Waller would be “inclined to support holding” rates where they are, arguing that recent readings “suggest we are finally seeing some signs of disinflation”. The odds on a September rise fell about twelve points to 54.6%, the 10-year yield dropped six basis points and the Dow had its best day in a month. Warsh hawkish, Williams sanguine, Goolsbee worried about overheating, Waller dovish — four public positions in six days, and the August jobs report lands this morning.

Economy 3

The hawkish consensus that formed at Jackson Hole lasted six days.

  • Fed governor Waller indicates he will support holding rates steady in September
    CNBC3 Sep

    He conceded inflation is “meaningfully above” 2% and argued the trend is finally turning. The 10-year fell about six basis points to 4.74% and hike odds dropped roughly twelve points. One governor is not a committee, but he is the first to put a dissent on the record since the chairman spoke.

  • Not so fast on rate hikes, some Fed officials say
    Axios3 Sep

    The plural is what matters. This is no longer Warsh against the market; it is the committee against itself, with a decision due on 15–16 September and an oil price set by a war neither side of the argument controls.

  • What to expect from Friday's jobs report — and AI in the future
    CNN3 Sep

    Consensus is 65,000 jobs added and unemployment ticking up to 4.2%, after July unexpectedly lost 23,000. The structural line underneath is the one worth keeping: for roughly three years, 94% of American job growth has come from just three sectors — healthcare, leisure and hospitality, and state and local government.

Jobless claims came in at 206,000 for the week to 29 August, close to the 205,000 expected, with continuing claims up 8,000 to 1.78 million. A low-hire, low-fire labour market, still.

AI 4

The rumour became a deal, and three of the biggest models went dark at the same time.

  • Nvidia is buying Hugging Face for nearly $13 billion
    Axios3 Sep

    $12.93bn, confirmed after a week of reports. Jensen Huang says the hub will keep running as an open platform and developers will remain free to choose their models, clouds and hardware. That promise is the whole question: the platform is now owned by the company whose hardware most of it runs on.

  • Nvidia to buy open-source AI hub Hugging Face for $12.9bn
    Euronews3 Sep

    The European read, and the reason it matters here: five days ago founders at a Copenhagen conference were arguing that Europe should own its models and infrastructure rather than rent them. The main open-source distribution point has just been bought by an American chipmaker.

  • Hugging Face goes from a 'scrappy' startup named after an emoji to a $13 billion acquisition
    Fortune3 Sep

    Useful for the context rather than the news. The buyer’s motive is defensive: OpenAI, Google and Anthropic are all now designing their own silicon, and owning the place developers go to download open models is a hedge against the day they stop needing Nvidia’s.

  • ChatGPT, Claude and Grok all went down at once
    The Register3 Sep

    A routing error took ChatGPT and Codex out for about half an hour on Thursday morning US time; Claude was degraded for three hours; Grok was affected too. Three unrelated companies failing together is a concentration story, not a coincidence — and worth noting on a page assembled by a tool of the same kind.

Iran War 0

Empty, and after this week that is itself worth reporting.

Two searches across Al Jazeera, CNN, France 24, CNBC and Euronews returned nothing filed on Thursday. Every story this brief carried on Wednesday — the strikes on southern Iran, the retaliation against bases in Jordan, Bahrain, Kuwait and Erbil, the rising casualty count, Trump’s threat to strike “anytime we want” — ran here the day it broke, and nothing was added to it yesterday.

The market read the same silence: oil was little changed for the first session this week, having risen every day since Sunday. One quiet day is not a ceasefire, and the underlying position has not moved — the strait stays shut, there are no direct US–Iran talks, and Oman and Qatar are still the only channels open. But a day without a strike is news in a week that had four.
