Story of the day

Saudi Arabia and the Houthis spent Tuesday attacking each other’s oil. The coalition struck a Yemen Oil Company site outside Sanaa, having first ordered it evacuated; hours later the Houthis hit an Aramco administrative building in Dhahran and threatened the rest. The evacuation warning is worth noticing: it is what a party does when it wants the infrastructure destroyed and the people alive, which makes this a deliberate campaign against energy capacity rather than a reprisal for the airport. In the fourth week of a global diesel shortage, with the IMF having just revised world inflation up to 4.7% on energy and food, the two governments best placed to relieve it have decided to burn each other’s refineries instead.

The long end came down, and the reason was the oil price rather than anything a central bank did.

  • 10-year Treasury yield falls as energy prices stabilise
    CNBC13 Oct

Economy 2

American inflation data lands today, and the forecast has energy doing all of the damage.

  • The latest inflation data is expected to show price increases are accelerating
    CNBC13 OctPreview

    A preview rather than a result: the September report had not appeared when this edition was built. Economists expect headline prices up 0.6% on the month and 3.6% over the year, against 3.4% in August — but core inflation at just 2.5%. That gap is the whole story. Underlying price pressure is close to target while the headline accelerates, because Brent rose more than 14% during September. A central bank cannot tighten its way out of a strait.

  • IMF raises its 2026 global growth forecast to 3.1% as economic inequality widens
    Fortune13 Oct

    The distributional reading of Monday’s outlook, and the one that will matter longest. The upgrade belongs to economies building AI capacity; the energy inflation it generates belongs to everyone. An average that improves while its spread widens is not a recovery, and the fund has now put that in its own forecast rather than in a footnote.

AI 5

A warning about self-replicating models, a flat refusal to believe it, and the company issuing both opening its books today.

  • Anthropic warns AI models could soon copy themselves across home devices
    CNBC13 Oct

    Published research putting small models “months away” from the capabilities needed to replicate across internet-connected devices in people’s houses. Taken at face value it is the most serious thing the company has said about its own products, and it arrives four days after it admitted it cannot reliably keep its agents inside a test environment. Read it alongside the next item rather than on its own.

  • Anthropic conjures another threat that doesn’t exist yet to stoke fears of AI apocalypse
    The Register13 Oct

    The specific objection, and it is a good one: the study demonstrates that a model could copy itself once it is inside a device, and skips the step of getting in. That step is the whole of computer security. The wider charge — that a firm about to sell shares benefits from being the one whose products are dangerous — is uncomfortable and is not refuted by the research being real. See the disclosure below.

  • Anthropic to disclose new revenue numbers as AI lab financials become central to the tech trade
    CNBC13 Oct

    Third-quarter figures to be shown to prospective investors today, for the first time, ahead of a planned listing. Last week a $20 billion restatement at OpenAI moved Nvidia, Oracle and CoreWeave on a day none of them reported — and the convention that made OpenAI’s number look larger was Anthropic’s own. Today the comparison runs the other way, with an audience that can price it.

  • Every major Nvidia customer is now a competitor as Microsoft reveals a new Maia chip
    Axios13 Oct

    The Maia 300 from 2027, intended as the primary inference system for Microsoft’s own products; OpenAI designing a chip with Broadcom; Anthropic exploring its own silicon since August. Inference is the recurring cost of running these models, so whoever owns that chip owns the margin. Nvidia’s customers are not diversifying away from it out of principle.

  • FBI arrests an OpenAI employee accused of planting a hidden recording device in a conference room
    TechCrunch13 Oct

    An employee was dismissed and arrested on 9 October over an alleged device in an executive meeting room; the allegation is untested and he has not been tried. OpenAI says it has found no sign that model weights, source code or customer data were improperly accessed or removed. Industrial espionage inside a frontier lab is a different threat model from the ones these companies publish papers about, and it is the one with a suspect in custody.

Iran War 4

Both sides are now going after oil infrastructure, and Saudi air defence is not stopping what is aimed at it.

  • Oil facility near the Yemeni capital Sanaa hit by Saudi Arabia, the Houthis say
    France 2413 Oct

    A Yemen Oil Company site, struck after the coalition ordered an evacuation, according to Houthi accounts; Saudi Arabia did not immediately respond to the claim. The warning is the notable part — it converts the strike from an attack on people into an attack on capacity, and it tells you the campaign expects to be judged on that distinction later.

  • Houthis strike Aramco hours after the Saudi-led coalition hits a Sanaa oil site
    Al Jazeera13 Oct

    An administrative building at Dhahran, according to Saudi officials, with threats against further Aramco facilities and airports. Dhahran is the company’s headquarters rather than a production site, so the damage is symbolic and the message is not: the group is saying it can reach the institution, not just the pipeline.

  • Why billions spent on defence haven’t shielded Saudi Arabia from Houthi attacks
    CNN13 OctAnalysis

    The question behind the week: one of the world’s largest arms importers cannot keep missiles and drones off its own capital’s airport. Interceptors cost orders of magnitude more than what they shoot down and there are never enough of them in the right place, which is the arithmetic every air-defence system in the world now faces. American support so far runs to more than a hundred military advisers under a new joint command.

  • Yemeni government launches air strikes on Houthi positions
    Al Jazeera13 Oct

    The ground and air campaign continuing alongside the exchange of strikes on energy sites, with Riyadh’s airport still shut and international airlines extending cancellations into next week. Two wars are now running in the same country — a Yemeni civil war over territory and a Saudi-Houthi war over infrastructure — and only the second one moves the oil price.
