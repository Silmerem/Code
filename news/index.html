Story of the day

Tehran has sent Washington its conditions for ending the war, by way of Doha — the first concrete diplomatic movement in weeks, and it arrives on the same Saturday that Donald Trump extended sanctions on Iran. Two days ago the president was publicly weighing whether to “annihilate” his Iranian foes; now there is a document in a Qatari channel. Nothing about its contents has been made public by either side, and a list of conditions is not a negotiation. But after a fortnight in which the only exchanges were missiles and tankers, a piece of paper is a change of instrument.

Finance 1

The week’s yield move has already invalidated the official forecasts for the next decade.

  • Treasury yields are already blowing up the CBO’s long-term forecasts
    Fortune19 Sep

    The Congressional Budget Office’s ten-year projections of federal interest costs assumed borrowing rates the market has now left behind, and economists who spent years dismissing American debt worries are revising. Ed Yardeni’s framing is that the ten-year may be breaking out above 5% rather than touching it — a distinction between an episode and a level.

One headline: markets were closed on Saturday and no reachable outlet filed a dated markets report.

Economy 2

A country reconsidering which bloc it belongs to, and two chip industry veterans doing the arithmetic on AI.

  • Canadians warm to the EU’s ‘associate membership’ proposal
    Al Jazeera19 Sep

    An Abacus Data survey puts support for closer integration with the European Union — easier transatlantic trade, travel, work and study, without joining — at 81%. That is a striking number for a country whose economy is built around a land border, and it is a measure of what the tariff war has done to Canadian assumptions rather than to Canadian trade figures.

  • Gelsinger and Rao: we have seen Silicon Valley move fast and break things — consider the maths of where we are headed
    Fortune19 SepOpinion

    An argued piece by two people who have run chip businesses, flagged as opinion. Their case is about compound arithmetic rather than sentiment: the capital and power commitments now being made only make sense on growth assumptions that have to hold for a decade, and nobody is required to state them out loud.

    Fortune19 Sep

    Announced on Saturday, with a nod to a law-enforcement role and a promise to appoint an AI czar; the calls for a slowdown were dismissed as politically driven “hoaxes”. “Rather, we will cherish it.” What a new uniformed service would do that existing agencies cannot was not set out.

  • As AI companies near ‘recursive self-improvement’, a physics professor calls full autonomy ‘the worst idea in the history of humanity’
