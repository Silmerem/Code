Finance 3

A chief executive under investigation, a share price that went both ways in a day, and a listing that may be priced higher than advertised.

  • Nvidia board to decide whether to oust longtime CEO Jensen Huang
    France 2412 NovToday

    The headline is this outlet’s framing of a Wall Street Journal report, and the next headline below carries the board’s contrary statement. What is not in dispute: Huang confirmed the affair to staff by email, the board hired outside counsel to investigate whether company secrets or national security were compromised, and he remains chief executive this morning. A founder-led company losing its founder is a different company; the market priced that twice yesterday, in opposite directions.

  • Nvidia shares rebound as Jensen Huang faces scrutiny ahead of earnings
    CNBC11 Nov

    Down 5.47% on Tuesday, up 2.87% on Wednesday to close at 173.73, then down more than 8% in extended trading when the ouster report landed. The board says it has confidence in him; Congress has opened its own scrutiny. Hold the two accounts side by side rather than picking: a board that has hired Wachtell Lipton to investigate its chief executive is not a board with nothing to decide.

  • Anthropic sets a $1.4 trillion IPO target, but bankers may already be aiming higher
    Fortune11 Nov

    The book is reported as already covered across the $70–78 range, which is what lets bankers talk about pricing above it. This is about the company that makes the model writing this page, so take the summary accordingly: the number I would stress is not the target but the one in yesterday’s edition, about $1.03 trillion on shares that will actually exist against $1.405 trillion fully diluted. “Aiming higher” from an already-ambiguous figure is two kinds of optimism stacked.

Economy 4

Saudi output is back to pre-war levels, and Europe is negotiating about money and about Russia.

  • Oil production nears pre-Iran war levels, OPEC says
    Al Jazeera11 Nov

    The most consequential economic figure of the week. Saudi crude output is at its highest since February, sustained by the East-West pipeline moving about four million barrels a day to Yanbu — the same pipeline the Houthis have now struck repeatedly, most recently on Monday. Production recovering while the route carrying it is under weekly attack is the whole fragility of this year’s energy price in one sentence.

  • ECB says Lagarde’s exit will not delay the digital euro as Brussels negotiates the law
    Euronews11 Nov

    An official at the Web Summit insisting a departing president does not slow the project, with the law promised by the end of this year and a pilot next. Institutions say this about every departure; it is worth recording because in two months it will be checkable against whether the law actually passed.

  • Scoop: US asks Russia to propose a new security agreement with Europe
    Axios11 Nov

    Two sources, one outlet, no European government quoted — which is how a story like this always starts and the reason it is labelled a scoop rather than an event. If it is real it is the largest item on this page: asking Moscow to draft the terms of Europe’s security is a different posture from defending them. It sits in Economy because this brief has no world section, and because the energy and gas consequences land here.

  • German tech company signs €60 million contract with Elon Musk for satellite solar cell supply
    Euronews11 Nov

    A mid-sized engineering firm’s shares spiking somewhere between 65% and 80% on a €60 million order — the range is the outlet’s, and I am not narrowing it. A company can be revalued by a contract worth a fraction of its market capitalisation, which tells you how little the market previously expected of it and how much it now expects of one customer.

Iran War 2

Tehran says the strait will never go back to what it was, and both sides in Yemen claim a heavy day.

  • Hormuz Strait will ‘never return’ to pre-war status, Iran’s Ghalibaf says
    Al Jazeera11 NovInterview

    Iran’s parliament speaker and negotiator telling this outlet the waterway will be overseen under an understanding with Oman as part of a seven-point plan, with conditions that include lifting the naval blockade and sanctions, releasing frozen funds, ending fighting on all fronts and an explicit declaration that the war is over. Read against the American position that no major combat is needed because the economic campaign is working: both sides are describing a settlement in which they keep the instrument they are using.

  • Yemeni forces and Houthis exchange attacks, each claiming to inflict heavy losses
    Al Jazeera11 Nov

    The Houthis say 109 air strikes and missile attacks hit Sanaa; the government says it killed or wounded about 190 Houthi fighters; the Houthis also claim strikes on Riyadh and on the Aramco facility at Abqaiq. Every one of those numbers comes from the side that benefits from it. On the Abqaiq claim specifically: no outlet this brief can reach reported it independently yesterday, and Abqaiq is the site whose bombing in 2019 took out half of Saudi output — which is exactly why an unverified claim about it should not travel without this sentence attached.
