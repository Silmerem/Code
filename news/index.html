Story of the day

The Treasury blinked. After three days of yields climbing to levels not seen since 2007, the department announced it would at least double the size of its debt buybacks — from $2bn to at least $4bn a go. The 30-year yield fell nine basis points to 5.195%, the 10-year more than five to 4.653%, and the S&P ended a three-day losing run. It is a real intervention with a real cost: buying back long paper shortens the maturity of a $32tn debt pile, which makes the interest bill more sensitive to every future rate rise.

Economy 2

The intervention, and the fight it starts between the Treasury and the Fed.

  • Treasury doubles debt buybacks as Bessent moves to steady the bond market
    CNBC19 Aug

    The maximum size of a buyback operation goes from $2bn to “at least” $4bn. Yields fell immediately and equity futures jumped. The caution from bond traders in the piece is the part worth keeping: this trades a lower yield today for a shorter average maturity, and a shorter maturity is what makes a debt stock re-price fast when rates rise.

  • Bessent moves to curb Treasury yields, putting new pressure on Warsh's Fed
    CNBC19 Aug

    The institutional story underneath the market one. Managing the long end of the curve has been the central bank’s territory; a Treasury secretary doing it directly, while the market prices a hike the Fed has not signalled, sets up the more consequential question of the autumn — who is actually setting US monetary conditions.

UK Politics 3

The first hard target of this premiership: rough sleeping ended by Christmas.

  • UK PM launches bid to get rough sleepers off the streets by Christmas
    France 2419 Aug

    £102m for emergency accommodation this winter, on top of the £340m announced last month for settled housing, and a promise of more than 1,000 permanent homes over three years. A first national summit on rough sleeping follows later in the year. “By Christmas” is the notable part — a date, publicly set, four months out, on a problem that has defeated every government that has named one.

  • Burnham to donate 15% of the PM's salary to homelessness: 'I want to lead from the front'
    Yahoo News UK19 Aug

    Told to the Big Issue alongside the funding announcement. It is a gesture rather than a policy and the sum is trivial against £442m — but it is the kind of gesture that makes a target personal, which is presumably the point of making it on the same day as the target.

  • Rayner defends Burnham's record on tackling rough sleeping in Manchester
    Yahoo News UK19 Aug

    The attack the announcement invited: Burnham promised to end rough sleeping in Greater Manchester by 2020 as mayor and did not. Rayner’s defence is the record of a decade against the record of a pledge — and it is the argument this government will have to keep winning every time it names a date.

Iran War 3

Tuesday's two missiles cost Iran a trading partner on Wednesday — and the target list may now reach Europe.

  • UAE suspends all trade and financial transactions with Iran
    CNN19 AugLive blog

    The Emirates cut every commercial and financial channel to Tehran over the two ballistic missiles it says were fired at its waters on Tuesday. Dubai has been Iran’s main route to the outside economy through every sanctions round of the past twenty years; closing it is a heavier blow than the missiles that caused it, which did no damage at all.

  • UAE imposes an indefinite trade embargo on Iran over alleged missile attacks
    Al Jazeera19 Aug

    The word doing the work here is “alleged”. Iran denies firing anything and calls it a false-flag operation. Nobody outside the two governments has verified the launch, and an indefinite embargo now rests on a claim only one side makes — worth holding lightly until something independent confirms it.

  • Iran reportedly weighs attacks on European military targets as the UAE severs trade
    CNBC19 Aug

    NATO said it is ready to “address any threat” after reports Tehran is considering strikes on US military targets in Europe should Washington escalate. Meanwhile the strait stays shut in practice whatever is claimed about it: traffic remains near nothing, and an attack on a cargo vessel this week killed one crew member.
