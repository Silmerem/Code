Story of the day

American consumer prices rose 0.4% in August and 3.4% over the year, both as forecast, but petrol alone accounted for more than a third of the monthly increase — up nearly 4% in the month and 27% on the year, to about $4.30 a gallon against $3.19 twelve months ago. Economy 4

The number came in exactly as forecast and made the Fed’s decision harder rather than easier.

  • Consumer prices rose 0.4% in August, putting annual inflation at 3.4%
    CNBC11 Sep

    Both figures matched estimates. The complication is underneath them: core prices rose 0.3% on the month, a little above expectation, while the annual core rate sits at 2.4% — a full point below the headline. The gap is the war, and it is the whole argument about whether to raise rates.

  • Here’s the inflation breakdown for August 2026 — in one chart
    CNBC11 Sep

    Petrol did more than a third of the monthly work on its own: up close to 4% in August and 27% over the year, at roughly $4.30 a gallon against $3.19 last September. Diesel reached $6 a gallon on Friday, a record, having already risen 24% at the wholesale level in a single month.

  • Even a cooler inflation report may not be enough to keep the Fed from raising rates next week
    CNN11 Sep

    The argument for moving anyway is about expectations rather than this month’s number: a committee that lets a supply shock run risks having it built into wage and price setting. The argument against is that raising rates does nothing to a blockade and everything to households already paying $4.30 a gallon.

  • Inflation stays hot in August as the Iran war drives energy costs higher
    Axios11 Sep

    The political reading, which is the one that will shape next week: an inflation rate driven by a war the administration is prosecuting, arriving before midterms in which support for that war is already at 31%. Monetary policy is about to be made inside that.
