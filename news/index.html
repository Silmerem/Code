Story of the day

Two fears were traded against each other yesterday and both of them won. The Nasdaq fell 2.6%, its worst day since June, on doubts about how much AI capital spending can be justified; at the same time the 10-year Treasury yield dropped more than 17 basis points to 4.972%, below 5% for the first time in a month and its sharpest one-day fall since March 2023, as money ran for cover. And it ran straight into oil, which rose 4% on a report that the United States and Israel have drawn up plans for renewed strikes on Iran after the 3 November midterms. One session, three prices, and the same question under all of them: what is a promise worth when nobody will say when it pays.

Finance 2

The AI trade sold off and the bond market caught the money.

  • US tech stocks tumble as investors fret over AI spending
    Al Jazeera23 OctToday

    The Nasdaq Composite down 2.6% to 26,008.86, its steepest fall since early June; the S&P 500 off 1.21%; the Dow almost unchanged. Nvidia fell about 4.4%. The split between the Dow and the Nasdaq is the whole story in one line: this was not a market losing confidence in the economy, it was a market losing patience with one trade inside it.

  • 10-year Treasury yield tumbles below 5% to its lowest level in nearly a month
    CNBC22 Oct

    Down more than 17 basis points to 4.972%, the first reading under 5% since 23 September and the largest one-day decline since March 2023. The two-year fell to 4.659%. On Wednesday this brief reported the same yield sitting at 5.109% and noted how little it had moved; a day later it made its biggest move in three and a half years. That is worth recording as a limit on what a morning snapshot can tell you.

Economy 3

Oil at a five-month high, one interest rate for three different inflations, and a French government banning protests.

  • Oil prices today: Brent settles at its highest since May
    CNBC22 Oct

    Brent up about 4% to settle at $113.17 and WTI up 2.4% to $99.47, after a New York Times report that American and Israeli planners have prepared a “short but intensive” operation against Iran to follow the 3 November midterms. Note what moved the price: not a strike, a report of a plan. Everything in the British and European inflation numbers below starts here.

  • One interest rate, three inflation realities: why Europe’s price shock is so uneven
    Euronews22 OctAnalysis

    September inflation at 2.7% in Finland, 3.3% in Germany and 6.1% in Lithuania, against a euro-area average of 3.8% — all of them governed by one deposit rate, now 2.50%. Energy explains close to half of the headline figure and core sits at 2.5%. The ECB meets on 28 and 29 October. A single rate for a 3.4-point spread is the monetary union’s oldest problem, and an energy shock is the condition that makes it worst.

  • Authorities in Lyon and Marseille ban all protests on Saturday
    France 2422 Oct

    Bans in two more cities after last weekend’s riots and looting, with night curfews in parts of Paris until Monday and the Louvre, Orsay and Picasso closed on Saturday. The justice minister says 2,700 people have been taken into custody. It sits in Economy rather than in a world section this brief does not have, because the protests are over the cost of living and education funding — the same budget that took French borrowing costs above 5% on Wednesday.

AI 4

Meta showed what the buildout costs, and a bug found by AI models is now being exploited at scale.

  • Meta stock sinks nearly 7% as AI spending pushes free cash flow negative
    CNBC22 Oct

    Revenue beat expectations and the shares fell anyway, on capital spending guided to $135–145 billion and free cash flow of minus $2.12 billion for the quarter. Its five-year credit default swaps reached record spreads, which is the bond market saying out loud what the equity market only hinted at. This is the number that set the tone for the whole session above.

  • Hackers pile on PHP2Shell as AWS reveals attacks began before disclosure
    The Register22 Oct

    Cloudflare reports millions of exploit attempts against the PHP flaw disclosed this week, and Amazon says it saw attackers probing for it four days before the patches shipped. The bug was found by researchers at Chainguard, OpenAI and Wiz, all of whom say they used AI models to find it. The same capability that found it is now the reason everyone knew where to look.

  • PHP blames supply chain security firm Chainguard for putting huge chunks of the web at risk
    The Register22 Oct

    The PHP project says Chainguard shipped patched builds ahead of the coordinated disclosure; Chainguard’s chief executive says people were already being hit and the call was to ship the fix. Both accounts can be true at once, which is what makes it a real argument rather than a blame story — and the first serious test of how disclosure etiquette holds up when machines find the bugs faster than committees can agree on a date.

  • PHP security flaw: why websites across Europe went dark, and what it means for you
    Euronews22 OctVideo

    The consumer-facing version of the same event, for anyone whose bank or council site stopped answering this week. Carried because it is the only account of this story written for people who do not run servers, and because the brief’s source rules needed a third newsroom in this section — both reasons are given rather than one.

UK Politics 2

Five days to the Budget, and the fight is over which taxes are ruled out.

  • Budget tax rises for the financial sector would be an ‘own goal’, says the CBI
    ITV News20 Oct

    The employers’ group arguing against the one revenue measure the chancellor has come closest to confirming: a rise in the surcharge on bank profits, reportedly from 3% to 8%. The CBI’s case is competitiveness. The counter-case is that banks are the sector most visibly helped by the rate rises making everyone else’s life harder, which is why the measure survives every round of lobbying.

  • John Healey rejects calls to raise capital gains tax at the Budget
    ITV News19 Oct

    One more option taken off the table before 28 October, alongside a refusal to rule out extra borrowing. Each ruling-out narrows what is left, and what is left is a bank surcharge, energy support described as targeted and temporary, and a gap the IFS puts at £35 billion. The arithmetic is not improved by removing items from it.

Both from ITV News, and both older than the rest of this edition — the section’s four-day window exists because ITV is the only datestamped British newsroom this brief can reach, and it published nothing political yesterday.

Iran War 3

Washington is thinning its embassy in Riyadh while planning the next round for after the midterms.

  • Attack on Saudi Arabia’s King Fahd airport kills two people
    Al Jazeera22 Oct

    A Saudi national and an Indian resident killed and eight wounded at Dammam on Wednesday evening, with operations resumed by Thursday and damage still being assessed. The Houthis claimed attacks on King Fahd and Abha airports, the defence ministry in Riyadh and Aramco facilities at Yanbu — the same Yanbu where French personnel have been deployed for over a week.

  • US pulls diplomats from Saudi Arabia as renewed Iran strikes loom
    CNBC22 Oct

    Non-emergency staff and families ordered out, two days after Britain and France put forces into the kingdom’s defence. Ordering dependants out is the least ambiguous signal a government sends about what it expects next, and it was given the same week two European allies arrived.

  • Why these Iowa Republican voters are wary of Trump resuming the Iran war after the midterms
    CNN22 OctReporting

    The domestic constraint on the plan that moved the oil price yesterday, reported from the president’s own side of the aisle rather than from the opposition. A war resumed three weeks after an election is a war whose timing has already been shaped by one; whether that timing survives contact with these voters is a different question from whether the planning is complete.
