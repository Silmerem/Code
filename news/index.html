Story of the day

Trump announced on Sunday that Russia and Ukraine had agreed an immediate energy ceasefire. Russia has not confirmed it. Zelenskyy said the announcement was unexpected, that Ukraine would stop hitting Russian refineries if Moscow stopped hitting Ukrainian energy, and then that Russia struck a Kharkiv power plant the same evening. Trump made a near-identical announcement last month, which neither party confirmed either. And yet both crude contracts fell more than 1% this morning and equities rose, reversing the spike that followed the attacks on Riyadh’s airport. The market has priced an agreement that one of its two parties says is already being broken, which is a fact about how thin the supply picture is rather than about diplomacy.

Finance 3

The Ukraine story is in this section rather than a world one because it moved the oil price, which is the only reason markets looked at it.

  • Stocks rise as oil prices tumble after Trump hails an ‘energy ceasefire’
    Euronews12 OctToday

    Both main crude contracts down more than 1% in Asian hours, undoing the rise that came with the Houthi strikes on Saudi Arabia, with equities higher behind it. Japan is shut for a holiday, which thins the tape. The move is a judgement that Russian and Ukrainian refineries matter more to supply than a Saudi airport does — defensible, and made on an announcement neither Moscow nor Kyiv has jointly confirmed.

  • Trump claims Ukraine and Russia have agreed to an energy ceasefire
    CNN11 Oct

    Announced on Truth Social. A White House official describes it as each side ceasing attacks on oil refineries, with power plants and grids a possible second phase — so the thing announced is narrower than the phrase. CNN separately reports that Washington could suspend intelligence sharing, and European purchases of American weapons for Ukraine, if Kyiv does not stop its deep strikes on Russian energy. That is the mechanism behind the agreement.

  • Zelenskyy says Russia violated the energy truce announced by Trump
    Al Jazeera11 Oct

    A power plant in Kharkiv hit the same evening, according to Kyiv, with no immediate word from the Kremlin. Zelenskyy’s position is conditional rather than agreed: Ukraine stops if Russia stops. Read the two halves together and what exists is an American announcement, a Ukrainian offer and Russian silence — three different things being reported as one deal.

Economy 2

The price of diesel is now openly reordering alliances.

  • Ukrainian MP calls the US-Russia diesel deal a ‘betrayal’
    NPR11 Oct

    Washington agreed on Friday to ease sanctions on Russian diesel temporarily, and the reaction from Kyiv and European capitals is the story: a country being asked to stop attacking Russian refineries while its principal backer arranges to buy their output. Whatever the merits, this is what a fuel shortage does to a coalition — it converts a shared position into a set of separate interests.

  • Trump bears down on Zelensky as Russia keeps up attacks on Ukraine
    Axios11 Oct

    The pressure read of the same weekend, including the president’s suggestion that Ukraine should get a new leader. The economic logic is explicit and worth stating plainly: Ukrainian strikes on Russian refineries raise the price of fuel in American filling stations three weeks before an American election, and that is the interest being asserted here.

Iran War 3

Riyadh’s airport was hit for a second day, and the coalition is calling it a war crime.

  • Yemen fighting expands as the Houthis attack a Saudi airport again: what to know
    Al Jazeera11 Oct

    The Houthi military spokesman Yahya Saree frames the strikes as a response to “brutal Saudi aggression” and warns that Saudi Arabia will bear responsibility for any failed interception — a claim that any missile falling on a city is the defender’s fault. The group had not claimed Saturday’s deadly strike, though a senior official wrote that they had shut the airport. Saudi Arabia, Egypt, Pakistan and Türkiye have condemned the attacks jointly.

  • Saudi-led coalition says the Houthi attack on Riyadh airport was a ‘war crime’ and vows a response
    Al Arabiya11 Oct

    The coalition promises to “respond decisively to this terrorist attack in accordance with the Customary International Humanitarian Law” — a sentence that invokes the law of war in the same breath as a reprisal, which is a tension rather than a formality. The Saudi government says at least four Saudi citizens and two Bangladeshis were among Saturday’s dead.

  • Passenger terminal at Riyadh airport hit again, a day after the deadly strike
    CBS News11 Oct

    A second strike on the same terminal, and a Houthi threat against Saudi airspace generally. Trump is weighing whether to join the Saudi campaign, having previously refused Riyadh’s requests to do so; Senator Chris Murphy’s objection — that the United States should not retaliate on Saudi Arabia’s behalf — is the shape the domestic argument will take if he does.

The CBS page carries no date in its address and is placed from the second strike it reports. Death tolls for Saturday differ between outlets: most say at least twelve killed and about three hundred wounded, one account says thirteen, and the Saudi government has so far named six of the dead by nationality. The lower figure is used above.
